Safari Sam's Amazing World

Three ways to lay out the main world, for children aged 6 and up. Each one has the same 21 gates, and the inside of a gate is the same in all three, so you only compare how the world is laid out. Start with Home and the Zoo: they are the fully built places.

Pick a main world

Option A · side-scrolling

Explorer Street

A long street you swipe sideways. The gates stand in a row: Hometown, then the Wild Lands, then a boardwalk by the sea, then a night path to space. Sam walks to the gate you tap and the doors swing open.

Good

  • Closest to what you described
  • Very easy to understand
  • A new gate just makes the street longer

Watch out

  • 21 gates is a long walk
  • You only see a few gates at a time

Option B · map from above

Safari Island

One island seen from above. Each place sits where it belongs in nature: town in the middle, mountains and caves to the north, jungle and forest to the west, savanna and desert to the east, ocean and a deep trench all around. Tap a place: Sam travels there and the map zooms in.

Good

  • Feels most like a world to explore
  • Everything visible at once
  • The map itself teaches where creatures live

Watch out

  • The biggest drawing job for the illustrators
  • On a phone you drag the map around
Open Safari Island →
Option C · scroll up and down

Top-to-Bottom World

The world cut open like a cake. You start in Hometown and scroll up through the wild lands, mountains and sky to space, or down under the ground on one side and deeper and darker into the sea on the other. A height bar shows where Sam is.

Good

  • Teaches “where does it live: high, low, deep?”
  • Scrolling up and down is natural on phones
  • Cosmos, the animal books and Plants all fit

Watch out

  • Feels more like a poster than free roaming
  • Hometown is a crowded row of six

Inside every gate

Room scene: Home

A doll's house cut open. The hamster in its cage, a spider in the corner, the cat on the sofa, a goldfish bowl, the canary by the kitchen window, a mouse hole, and the dog by its kennel in the backyard.

Walk-along scene: Zoo

A wide zoo path you swipe along, past 12 enclosures: big cats, elephants, a savanna yard, gorillas, pandas, polar bears, penguins, kangaroos, a river pool, flamingos, a camel and a rainforest house.

Quick scenes: the other 19

Built straight from the creature list: land, greenhouse, ice, water, sky, cave, underground and space. In the Deep Sea you move a flashlight around to find the creatures.

Tap any creature: it jumps, a card shows the book's question and fact, then Why did God do that? answers Because He liked it!

The 21 places

Emoji are stand-ins for the books' illustrations. Sam is a simple stand-in drawing too.

The facts come from the compiled book notes. 371 of the 495 entries have only one fact; the plan suggests what to merge.
