## Summary

*Where Did Grandpa Go?* is the companion to the Grandma book: a short rhyming picture book for a child grieving a grandfather. It is by Gerald Robison (copyright 2026, Parrhesia Publishing, ISBN 978-1-971546-32-2 hardcover and 978-1-971546-33-9 paperback). The artist isn't credited in this file, though the style matches Fred Sayers' Grandma book.

- **Story:** A young child (drawn as a boy with brown hair, a green shirt and a toy train) looks everywhere for Grandpa while relatives gather after his death. No one will explain. The boy learns Grandpa has died and asks where he is.
- **Resolution:** A winged angel appears, tells the boy he has taken Grandpa to "a very special place" and is placing him "somewhere deep down inside," in a treasured place made of Grandpa's memories, hugs and kisses. The boy realizes: "the angel hid him in my heart."
- **What's new:** In this book Grandma is alive and grieving, and a picture shows Grandpa in heaven, which the Grandma book never shows. It also has a full Parent's Guide for the adult reading the book.

## Detailed description

**Front matter (PDF pages 1–8)**
- Pages i–ii are blank, and page iii is the title "Where Did Grandpa Go?" in yellow bubble letters.
- Page vii is a **Parent's Guide to Help Small Children in a Difficult Time.** It says small children mix truth with fantasy when someone dies, so they need "comfort and solace for their heart and soul" more than doctrine. It says "it may be easy to say a loved one went to heaven, it's certainly not easy to say they didn't, even if it might be true." It quotes 1 Corinthians 13:11 ("When I was a child, I spoke like a child…") and compares the book to "children's dosage" medicine: "small children need small doses of big medicines." It tells the reader to sit with the child, go slowly, ask questions ("Did you and ___ have special places and memories?"), let them ask questions and share memories, and close with prayer. It's signed "With gratitude, Gerald Robison."
- Page viii is the copyright page.

**Layout:** From the story on, pages come in pairs. An even page is a full-page picture with no words, and the next odd page is a framed block of rhyming text.

## Areas for curiosity

1. **What does "died" mean?** The boy hears "the doctors took him" and "God took him home" and nods without understanding. Ask what the child thinks it means.
2. **Why did the grown-ups say "go play"?** Ask how it feels to be left out.
3. **Grandma's tears (page 10).** The boy sees how sad Grandma is. Ask how he could help her and how she could help him.
4. **Places and objects.** The treehouse, lake, workshop, armchair and toy train all hold memories. Ask the child to name their own.
5. **"In spirit," "planted like a seed," "far, high or low."** The boy tries three ideas for where Grandpa is. Ask which makes sense to the child.
6. **Heaven in the picture.** Page 20 shows heaven, though the text never names it. Ask what the child sees and how it makes them feel.
7. **The angel's answer.** Grandpa is in a "special place" and in the boy's heart. Ask the child to say in their own words how both can be true.
8. **The title question.** Page 1 asks and page 25 answers.

## How it compares with the Grandma book

- **Nearly identical text, new details.** The couplets are the same, with Grandpa's details swapped in: garage and workshop instead of kitchen and sewing room, "straw and hay" instead of a window, and the extra couplets on pages 15 and 17 ("Has he gone far? Has he gone high? Or is he way down low?" and "Come, little one — no need to be a wreckage").
- **A heaven picture.** The Grandma book never shows where Grandma went, and this one does (page 20).
- **A winged angel.** In the Grandma book the "angel" is drawn like Jesus. Here the pictures show a clearly winged angel (and a statue).
- **A real parent's guide.** The Grandma book reuses the series' general letter, and this one has the full grief-specific guide.
- **Different layout.** Pictures and text sit on separate pages here (spreads), and share each page in the Grandma book.
- **Grandma is the mourner.** In the Grandpa book the grieving spouse is on the page.

## Things you may want to check

1. **Parent's guide wording.** "It may be easy to say a loved one went to heaven, it's certainly not easy to say they didn't" can be read as unclear. The guide also names heaven but the story's answer is "in your heart" (with a picture of heaven on page 20). Both answers are offered, so the message is mixed.
2. **Typos.**
   - Page 13: "Where has my Grandpa's gone?"
   - Page 17: "no need to be a wreckage" (probably a rhyme for "message").
   - Page 7: "Bye." in quotes with a period inside, and "eye" for "eyes."
   - Page 23: a stray closing quotation mark after "missing."
   - Page 25: "heart.!"
3. **Pictures versus text.** Page 3's text lists the garage and workshop, while the page 2 picture shows a treehouse, cellar doors and a lake, so the pictures and text don't match line for line.
4. **Same questions as the Grandma book** about the "angel" and the faith-specific message apply here too.
