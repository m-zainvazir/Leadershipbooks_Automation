I read all 101 pages of *Because He Liked It: Cosmos* (by Dr. Gerald Robison, illustrated by Jordan Blackstone). The text layer was clean, so I used it directly. Unlike the animal books, this one has no animals and no speech-bubble jokes. Each page is a "Why did God do that?" header and a short fact about space or Earth, so the "creatures" in this file are objects and topics. Each fact is written as a short question and answer.

The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Topics that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives.

## 🌌 Deep space: galaxies and the universe

**Creature: Galaxies (general)**  
Page: 1  
The question: How many galaxies might there be?  
The answer: There may be 400 billion stars in just our galaxy, and scientists guess there could be as many as 2 trillion galaxies.  
Page: 61 (Sloan Great Wall)  
The question: What is the largest structure ever found?  
The answer: The Sloan Great Wall, a chain of galaxy clusters 1.37 billion light-years long, made of superclusters and filaments of galaxies.  
Page: 63 (Great Barred Spiral Galaxy, NGC 1365)  
The question: Which galaxy has straight "bars"?  
The answer: Its arms and the wings at the ends of the arms look like straight bars, which makes it unusual and easy to spot.  
Page: 75  
The question: What is a galaxy?  
The answer: A group of stars, dark matter, gas and dust held together by gravity. Galaxies come in different shapes and sizes, from dwarfs with a few million stars to giants with trillions.  
Page: 78 (Globular cluster)  
The question: What is a globular cluster?  
The answer: A ball-shaped group of hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of stars bound together by gravity.

**Creature: Milky Way**  
Page: 26  
The question: How many stars and habitable planets are in our galaxy?  
The answer: Between 200 and 400 billion stars, and researchers estimate about 2 billion of the planets around them could support life.  
Page: 67  
The question: How fast are we really moving?  
The answer: Earth rotates at about 1,000 mph and orbits the sun at about 67,000 mph. The sun moves at about 43,500 mph, our solar system orbits the Milky Way at about 514,000 mph, and the Milky Way rotates at 600,000 mph.  
Page: 84  
The question: Will we need a new way to measure space?  
The answer: There are billions of stars in our own galaxy and billions of galaxies, and scientists may need a new way to measure such distances.

**Creature: Dark matter, dark energy and dark flow**  
Page: 41 (Dark matter)  
The question: Why don't galaxies fly apart?  
The answer: There isn't enough visible mass to hold them together, so scientists suspect more matter is present than we can see and call it "dark matter."  
Page: 42 (Dark energy)  
The question: Is the universe slowing down?  
The answer: No. In 1998 scientists found it is growing faster than ever, and they call whatever is speeding it up "dark energy."  
Page: 43 (Dark flow)  
The question: Where are all the galaxies going?  
The answer: They seem to move toward a certain point, like bathwater toward a drain. Scientists call what is moving them "dark flow."  
Page: 44  
The question: Will we all "go down the drain"?  
The answer: Scientists don't yet know whether all galaxy clusters are affected. The theory is that something we can't see is pulling on everything we see.

**Creature: Hole in the universe**  
Page: 60  
The question: Is there really a hole in the universe?  
The answer: A satellite mapping leftover heat found a cold spot where searches turn up no matter at all. Scientists have no explanation.

**Creature: Light-year**  
Page: 81  
The question: How do we measure space?  
The answer: An AU (about 93 million miles) is used inside the solar system, and a light-year is used beyond it. A light-year is almost 6 trillion miles, about 95,000 years in one of the fastest jets.  
Page: 82  
The question: Could you ever get there?  
The answer: No. At 95,000 years for one light-year, you, your children and your great-great-great-great-great-grandchildren wouldn't arrive.  
Page: 83 (Proxima Centauri)  
The question: How far is the nearest star?  
The answer: Proxima Centauri is 4.5 light-years away, over 400,000 years by the world's fastest jet. That's why scientists talk in light-years instead of miles.

## ☀️ The Sun and stars

**Creature: Sun**  
Page: 14  
The question: Is the sun a ball of fire?  
The answer: No. Fire needs heat, oxygen and something combustible. The sun is glowing gas powered by nuclear fusion.  
Page: 28  
The question: Can the sun become a black hole?  
The answer: No, it doesn't have enough mass. It would have to grow about 20 times its size.  
Page: 30  
The question: How hot is the sun?  
The answer: The surface is about 10,000°F and the center may reach more than 27 million degrees.  
Page: 31  
The question: Why isn't the sun too bright?  
The answer: Earth's atmosphere reflects about 30% of the sunlight back into space.  
Page: 32  
The question: How much energy does the sun give Earth?  
The answer: In just 1 hour, Earth receives more energy from the sun than fossil fuels use in an entire year.  
Page: 36  
The question: How long does sunlight take to reach us?  
The answer: About 8 minutes. If the sun turned off, we wouldn't know for 8 minutes.

**Creature: Brown dwarfs**  
Page: 38  
The question: What are failed stars?  
The answer: Stars called brown dwarfs, some of which are hardly warm enough to bake a potato.

**Creature: Betelgeuse**  
Page: 55  
The question: How big is this red supergiant?  
The answer: It's known for its huge size and red color. Scientists recently found two unexplained bright spots on it, as far apart as the sun is from Earth. Placed where our sun is, it would swallow the earth.

**Creature: Brightest star**  
Page: 62  
The question: What is the biggest, brightest known star?  
The answer: A star in a galaxy next to the Milky Way, about 265 times the mass of our sun and about 10 million times as bright.

**Creature: Black hole**  
Page: 28 (Our sun)  
The question: Can the sun become one?  
The answer: No. See "Sun" above.  
Page: 56  
The question: Do black holes dance?  
The answer: Like two magnets about to pull together, researchers found 33 pairs of supermassive black holes that have begun or will begin to "dance" at about 500,000 mph, even though they may be 3,000 to 8,000 light-years apart.  
Page: 76  
The question: What is a black hole?  
The answer: A region in space that acts like a super-strong vacuum cleaner. A star pulled in spirals like water down a drain and heats to millions of degrees, and its glow is the clue that a black hole is nearby.  
Page: 77 (Gamma-ray bursts)  
The question: Do gamma rays make a Hulk?  
The answer: No. Gamma-ray bursts are the most powerful explosions in the universe, happening when a massive star collapses. They can destroy almost anything nearby and may cause a black hole.

**Creature: Sound in space**  
Page: 40  
The question: What does space sound like?  
The answer: Mostly quiet, since sound can't travel in a vacuum. An exploding star could send out a shock wave (up to 20,000 miles per second) carrying material that sound could travel through, and it would be incredibly loud.

## 🪐 Planets, moons and the solar system

**Creature: WASP-17b**  
Page: 5  
The question: How hot can a planet be?  
The answer: It's a gas world with a daily temperature of about 2,700°F.

**Creature: Quartz-cloud world**  
Page: 6 (Unnamed, 1,300 light-years away)  
The question: Can clouds be made of rock?  
The answer: On a world 1,300 light-years away, quartz forms in the air, like quartz clouds. Its atmosphere is like WASP-17b's.

**Creature: CoRoT-7b**  
Page: 54  
The question: Can a planet evaporate?  
The answer: Scientists have watched this fiery molten planet slowly lose mass as its sun heats it and pulls it closer ("tidal migration"), so it seems to be disappearing.

**Creature: Mars mountain**  
Page: 12  
The question: How tall is the tallest mountain on Mars?  
The answer: Its base is about the size of Arizona and it is over 16 miles tall, against over 5 miles for Everest. Standing on top, you wouldn't be able to tell you were on a mountain.  
Page: 25  
The question: How tall is the tallest mountain on Mars?  
The answer: The same text word for word as page 12.

**Creature: Jupiter**  
Page: 24  
The question: Why does Jupiter have so many volcanoes?  
The answer: It's the largest planet in the solar system and has 95 moons, and their gravity pulls the planet in different directions, which the page gives as the reason for its volcanoes.

**Creature: Europa**  
Page: 73  
The question: Could there be life on Europa?  
The answer: It's an icy moon of Jupiter with an ocean below the ice. The surface is −256°F, but below the ice it's warmer, and life could cling to the underside of the ice.

**Creature: Saturn**  
Page: 64 (Prometheus)  
The question: Where can you find giant snowballs?  
The answer: The potato-shaped moon Prometheus pulls on ice particles in one of Saturn's rings, making snowballs up to 12 miles across.  
Page: 65 (Rings of gas giants)  
The question: Do other planets have rings?  
The answer: Yes. Jupiter, Uranus and Neptune have rings too. Uranus was thought to have 5 but has 13.  
Page: 101 (Hexagonal storm)  
The question: What is Saturn's strange storm?  
The answer: A nearly permanent hexagon-shaped storm around its north pole, about 18,000 miles wide, with clouds that change from deep blue to gold and beige.

**Creature: Uranus**  
Page: 16  
The question: How old would you be on Uranus?  
The answer: The page says it takes 21 years to go around the sun, so someone who died at 84 would be only 4 years old there.  
Page: 17  
The question: How does Uranus spin?  
The answer: It's tilted sideways and spins like a ball rolling downhill or somersaults, and the planet is more like a giant ball of ice.

**Creature: Kuiper belt and Oort cloud**  
Page: 57 (Kuiper belt)  
The question: What lies out past Neptune?  
The answer: A donut-shaped belt of space debris, ice, rocks and dwarf planets starting beyond Neptune and Pluto and extending 55 AU (55 × 93 million miles).  
Page: 74 (Oort cloud)  
The question: What is beyond the Kuiper belt?  
The answer: The Oort Cloud, a gigantic sphere around the whole solar system that extends thousands of AUs beyond the planets.

**Creature: Neptune and Pluto**  
Page: 79  
The question: Could Neptune and Pluto collide?  
The answer: No. In 3-D, Pluto's path is tilted 17 degrees, so the paths don't cross, and at their closest they're still over a billion miles apart.

**Creature: Solar system scale**  
Page: 80  
The question: How far apart are the planets really?  
The answer: If Earth were the size of a pea, Jupiter would be over 1,000 ft away and Pluto 1.5 miles away, too small to see without a microscope.

**Creature: Moon**  
Page: 18  
The question: How big is the moon?  
The answer: The page says Australia is bigger than the moon.  
Page: 89  
The question: Is the moon moving?  
The answer: Yes, away from Earth by about 3.8 cm a year, about as fast as your fingernails grow.  
Page: 94  
The question: What does the moon do for Earth?  
The answer: It slows Earth's spin. Without it our days might be only 6 hours long, and it also moderates winds and tides.

**Creature: Meteors and asteroids**  
Page: 7 (Space dust)  
The question: Where does dust come from?  
The answer: About 40,000 tons settle on Earth each year, left behind by asteroids and comets or from farther out in space.  
Page: 66 (Meteors)  
The question: Why do shooting stars have colors?  
The answer: They aren't stars. Friction makes the light, and the color depends on the meteor's makeup (magnesium makes blue-green, calcium makes violet) and its speed.  
Page: 91  
The question: How often do space rocks hit Earth?  
The answer: About 17,000 meteorites hit each year, but small ones burn up. Large asteroids are too big to burn up and leave craters, including the one thought to have killed the dinosaurs.

**Creature: Coldest place in space**  
Page: 69 (Boomerang Nebula)  
The question: Where is the coldest natural place in the universe?  
The answer: A gas and dust cloud 5,000 light-years away, kept at −458°F by a dying star at its center.  
Page: 29 (Space in general)  
The question: How cold is space?  
The answer: The page says the average temperature in empty space is −518°F.

**Creature: Alcohol cloud**  
Page: 71  
The question: Is there alcohol in space?  
The answer: A cloud of alcohol beyond our atmosphere is over 1,000 times the diameter of the solar system, with enough for 300,000 pints of beer for everyone on earth every day for a billion years.

## 🌍 Earth: the planet as a whole

**Creature: Earth (shape, spin and speed)**  
Page: 9  
The question: Is the earth ever still?  
The answer: It never stands still. It spins, orbits the sun, and keeps changing shape from earthquakes, volcanoes, shifting plates and the pull of the sun and moon (tides).  
Page: 85  
The question: Is Earth a perfect ball?  
The answer: No. It bulges at the equator because of its spin.  
Page: 86  
The question: Are days getting longer?  
The answer: Yes, by about 1.7 milliseconds each century.  
Page: 35  
The question: How wide is the earth?  
The answer: The radius at the equator is 4,025 miles.

**Creature: Earth's core**  
Page: 11  
The question: Where does life live?  
The answer: All known life lives in the top 25 miles of the surface, only about 1% of the earth, because the core is molten metal under huge pressure.  
Page: 34  
The question: Is anything on earth hotter than the sun?  
The answer: Yes, the core is 12,600°F and the sun's surface is 10,000°F.  
Page: 68  
The question: Why is the core still hot?  
The answer: Not because of an endless fire, but because radioactive isotopes release a steady stream of energy that turns to heat.

**Creature: Earth's magnetic field**  
Page: 92  
The question: What does the magnetic field do?  
The answer: It moves west about 10 miles a year and protects us from particles from the sun. Some scientists think the poles may swap, which would expose us to more meteorites and asteroids and confuse migrating animals.

**Creature: Earth's three conditions for life**  
Page: 39  
The question: What does a planet need for life?  
The answer: It must be in the "goldilocks" zone (not too far or too close to its sun), the right size, and have the right chemicals like oxygen, carbon dioxide and water.

**Creature: Earth's water**  
Page: 93  
The question: Is there water elsewhere in our solar system?  
The answer: Earth is the only planet in our solar system with water on its surface, nearly three-fourths of it.  
Page: 95  
The question: How much water can we drink?  
The answer: About 70% of Earth is water, but only 2.5% of that is fresh, and most of that is frozen at the poles.

**Creature: Earth's overpopulation**  
Page: 8  
The question: Is Earth overpopulated?  
The answer: No. People just live in big clusters. All of us could fit in Los Angeles, which could hold 12 billion people.

**Creature: Phytoplankton**  
Page: 10  
The question: Where does our oxygen come from?  
The answer: Trees make some, but tiny ocean phytoplankton (over 20,000 per drop of water) make one out of every 5 breaths you take.

**Creature: Gold**  
Page: 2  
The question: How much gold is in the earth?  
The answer: Most is in the core, enough to coat the whole earth with 18 inches of gold.  
Page: 37  
The question: How heavy is gold?  
The answer: A 1-liter milk jug full of gold weighs about 42 pounds, and the biggest piece found weighed 158 pounds.  
Page: 90  
The question: Is there gold in the ocean?  
The answer: Yes, but only about 1 gram per 100 million tons of water, so getting it costs more than it's worth.

**Creature: Diamond**  
Page: 52  
The question: Are diamonds forever?  
The answer: No. A diamond is compressed carbon and can burn. In a house fire, melted gold may survive, but the diamonds will be gone.

## 🌋 Land and geology

**Creature: Tectonic plates**  
Page: 27  
The question: Why do the continents move?  
The answer: The plates are like pans moving away from each other. They move up to 20 times faster where the crust is thin, but still only about as fast as your fingernails grow, which is why we don't feel it.

**Creature: Volcano**  
Page: 13 (Ring of Fire)  
The question: Where are the volcanoes?  
The answer: About 1,350 are active. Most form the Ring of Fire in the ocean, with 90% of earthquakes and 75% of volcanoes. Volcanoes also build new islands.  
Page: 46 (Basalt columns)  
The question: Why do some lava rocks look man-made?  
The answer: Cooling lava can contract into vertical hexagon-shaped columns, like the Giant's Causeway in Ireland and the Devil's Tower in Wyoming.

**Creature: Mount Everest**  
Page: 23  
The question: Is Everest still growing?  
The answer: Yes, about 4 mm (1/5 of an inch) a year, so about 4–5 years to grow an inch.

**Creature: Mammoth Cave**  
Page: 4  
The question: How big is the cave?  
The answer: A Kentucky cave over 400 miles long with its own weather and a constant 73°F. Warm air entering forms clouds, and it can rain inside the cave.

**Creature: Great Barrier Reef**  
Page: 87  
The question: What is the largest structure on Earth?  
The answer: Not the Burj Khalifa or the Tesla giga-factory, but Australia's Great Barrier Reef, covering 348,000 square kilometers.

**Creature: Tibetan Plateau**  
Page: 88  
The question: Does Earth have a third pole?  
The answer: In a way. Its glaciers, snow and permafrost feed several major Asian rivers and affect Earth's weather like the North and South poles.

**Creature: Death Valley moving rocks**  
Page: 45  
The question: How do the rocks move?  
The answer: Rocks weighing hundreds of pounds move hundreds of yards and leave trails. The page says scientists can't explain it, though some think wind and ice.

**Creature: Chocolate Hills**  
Page: 98  
The question: What are the Chocolate Hills?  
The answer: A Philippine landscape covered in hills that look like a box of chocolates when the grass turns brown in summer. They aren't made of chocolate.

**Creature: Zone of Silence**  
Page: 99  
The question: Where do radios go silent?  
The answer: A 1,500-square-mile area in Mexico where something interferes with radio frequencies and communication equipment. It hasn't been explained.

**Creature: Dancing forest**  
Page: 100  
The question: What is the "dancing forest"?  
The answer: A forest in Russia where trunks and limbs are twisted into odd shapes, as if frozen mid-dance. It isn't yet explained.

**Creature: Wallace Line**  
Page: 97  
The question: What invisible line do animals avoid?  
The answer: The Wallace Line separates the Asian and Australian plates and is also a climate and biological boundary that animals don't tend to cross. Scientists don't know why.

**Creature: Mississippi River**  
Page: 3  
The question: How long does it take water to flow down?  
The answer: The river runs about 2,340 miles, and a raindrop at the head takes nearly 3 months to reach the bottom.

## 🌊 Oceans and ice

**Creature: Ocean**  
Page: 19 (Underwater waterfall)  
The question: Where is Earth's biggest waterfall?  
The answer: Under the ocean between Greenland and Iceland, where cold dense water sinks nearly 2 miles beneath warmer water. It's over 100 miles wide.  
Page: 22  
The question: How much of the ocean is mapped?  
The answer: Only 19%, so most of it is unexplored.  
Page: 47 (Blue holes)  
The question: What are blue holes?  
The answer: Places where the ocean floor suddenly drops several hundred feet, turning the water dark blue. They lack oxygen and have little life.  
Page: 48 (Red tide)  
The question: What is red tide?  
The answer: A flood of colored single-cell algae turns the sea bright red. A little is harmless, but a lot can be harmful or fatal.  
Page: 58 (Currents)  
The question: How much water moves around the globe?  
The answer: About 321 million cubic miles a year. The Antarctic current moves 468 billion tons of water every second.  
Page: 59  
The question: What drives the currents?  
The answer: Differences in temperature and salt. About 400 billion tons of water an hour move from the warm equator toward Europe, giving off warmth along the way.  
Page: 96 (Mariana Trench)  
The question: How deep is the deepest place?  
The answer: Nearly 7 miles, deeper than the tallest mountain is tall, with 1,100 times the pressure at sea level.

**Creature: Ice circles**  
Page: 49  
The question: How do perfect ice circles form?  
The answer: Spinning, freezing water attracts bits of ice and floating material and forms a perfect ice circle, some up to 500 ft across.

**Creature: Antarctica**  
Page: 15 (Coldest place on Earth)  
The question: Where is Earth's coldest spot?  
The answer: A high ridge in East Antarctica, at −136°F.  
Page: 72 (Lake Vostok)  
The question: Is there a lake under Antarctica?  
The answer: Lake Vostok is under the ice and one of the 15 largest lakes on Earth. It's about 27°F but doesn't freeze because of the pressure of the ice above.

**Creature: Arctic Circle**  
Page: 20  
The question: Why might you miss the Arctic Circle?  
The answer: It keeps moving, about 49 feet a year, because of Earth's tilt, the pull of the sun and moon, volcanoes and earthquakes.

## ⛈️ Weather and sky

**Creature: Lightning**  
Page: 21  
The question: How often does lightning strike?  
The answer: About 100 times every second.  
Page: 70  
The question: What makes thunder?  
The answer: Lightning heats the air to about 5,000°F, which expands and vibrates, and that's thunder.

**Creature: Temperature records**  
Page: 33  
The question: How hot and how cold has Earth been?  
The answer: The highest was 136°F in 1922 and the lowest −128°F in 1983, 264 degrees apart.

**Creature: Mammatus clouds**  
Page: 50  
The question: What do clouds tell us?  
The answer: Mammatus clouds hang below other clouds because they form from sinking air, are usually dark, and mean a storm is coming.

**Creature: Fire rainbows**  
Page: 51  
The question: What are fire rainbows?  
The answer: When the sun, place and time are just right, crystals in clouds refract light into a spectrum of color. They're rare.

**Creature: Weather forecasting**  
Page: 53  
The question: Why can we predict sunrise but not the weather?  
The answer: Sunrise follows Earth's regular rotation and orbit, but weather depends on many things, including orbital mechanics, volcanoes, thermal springs, the sun and human activity.

## What the book shows overall

- **This book is a "wonders of Earth and space" collection.** It moves in order of scale: the universe and galaxies, then the sun and planets, then Earth's core and surface, and finally oddities like the Zone of Silence and the dancing forest.
- **It is full of record-setting numbers:** 2 trillion galaxies, a 1.37-billion-light-year Sloan Great Wall, a Mars mountain over 16 miles tall, and an ocean waterfall over 100 miles wide.
- **It likes mystery.** Pages 41–44 (dark matter, dark energy, dark flow), 45 (moving rocks), 60 (hole in the universe), 97 (Wallace Line), 99 (Zone of Silence) and 100 (dancing forest) all end with "scientists don't know."
- **It talks back to earlier pages.** Page 9 says "remember reading about [tectonic plates] earlier?", and page 52 begins "Earlier, you learned about how much gold is in the earth."
- **There are no animal jokes here.** The only humor is in the text itself, like "Kuiper belt is not what holds up Kuiper's pants" (p57) and the Hulk page (p77).

## Things you may want to check

1. **Duplicate page.** Pages 12 and 25 are word for word the same (the Mars mountain). Page 12 says "You've already read about Mt Everest," but Everest isn't until page 23, so page 12 looks like a misplaced copy of page 25.
2. **Facts that look wrong.**
   - Page 16 says Uranus takes 21 years to orbit the sun. It takes about 84 Earth years, so a person who lived to 84 would be about 1 year old there, not 4.
   - Page 29 says space is −518°F. That's below absolute zero (−459.67°F), and page 69 gives −458°F as the coldest place in the universe.
   - Page 15 gives −136°F as Earth's coldest and page 33 gives −128°F, so the two pages disagree.
   - Page 18 says Australia is bigger than the moon. Australia is wider than the moon is across, but the moon has more surface area, so this is misleading.
   - Page 24 gives Jupiter's volcanoes, but the volcanic body is its moon Io, and Jupiter has more than 95 moons now.
   - Page 35 gives Earth's equatorial radius as 4,025 miles. The usual figure is about 3,963 miles.
   - Page 45 says scientists can't explain the moving Death Valley rocks. A 2014 study explained them as thin ice sheets pushed by wind.
   - Page 58's 468 billion tons of water per second looks far too large for the Antarctic current.
   - Page 70 says lightning heats air to 5,000°F and calls that 5 times the sun's surface, but page 30 gives the sun's surface as 10,000°F. Lightning is nearer 50,000°F.
   - Page 22's "19% mapped" is an older figure.
   - Page 68 says the core's heat is from radioactive isotopes. Leftover heat from Earth's formation also contributes.
3. **Typos I noticed.** "artic" and "it's" for "its" (p20), "Antartica" (p15), "Burge Khalifa" (p87), "volcanos" (p13), and "milky way" without capitals (p67).
4. **OCR limits.** The text was clean, so I did not need page images for this book. I did not look at the illustrations, so I can't confirm the captions drawn inside them (for example, the unreadable caption on page 84).
