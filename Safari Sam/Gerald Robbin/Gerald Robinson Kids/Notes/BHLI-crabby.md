I read all 101 pages of the *Because He Liked It!* book in the file named "crabby interior" (the cover title is printed twice on top of itself, so the OCR can't read it; I'm calling it "Crabby" after the file name and the sand-bubbler crab on page 1). Each page follows the usual pattern: a "Why did God do that?" header, a short fact about an animal, and a joke caption from the animal in a speech bubble. The answer is always "Because He liked it!"

About 60 pages had scrambled OCR text, so I rendered the scrambled pages as images and read them directly. The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Creatures that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives.

## 🐾 Land: mammals

**Creature: Kangaroo**  
Page: 2  
The question: Do kangaroos really box?  
The answer: Yes, boxing seems to be part of their nature. Years ago a sideshow might have a man box a kangaroo, and since kangaroos can also use their very powerful hind feet, the match could be vicious.

**Creature: Warthog**  
Page: 3  
The question: Why "warthog," and how many tusks does it have?  
The answer: The name comes from the wart-like bumps on its face. It's dangerous and can kill with its tusks. It has 4, two on each side, while an elephant has only two.

**Creature: Panda**  
Page: 5  
The question: How does a panda hold bamboo?  
The answer: It has an elongated wrist-bone that sticks out and uses it like a thumb to hold the bamboo shoot.

**Creature: Bull**  
Page: 8  
The question: Why does a matador use a red cape?  
The answer: The bull can't see the color red, only the motion. The red cape is more for the audience than for the bull.

**Creature: Elephant**  
Page: 12  
The question: Where do some elephants go at night?  
The answer: To an underground cave (the page calls it the "elephant basement") that is cool and safe, with salty walls they lick for calcium, sodium and magnesium.  
Page: 26  
The question: How is elephant skin different from ours?  
The answer: It doesn't flake off. Older skin gets harder and builds up in layers, so elephants need water poured on them to soak in. Their only sweat glands are between their toes, so they also use their ears as fans.

**Creature: Echidna**  
Page: 13  
The question: What kind of animal is a spiny anteater?  
The answer: It looks like an upside-down scrub brush, with quills, a long snout that is both mouth and nose, a long sticky tongue for ants and termites, and strong back legs for digging. It's a mammal that lays eggs and keeps them in a pouch.

**Creature: Bighorn sheep**  
Page: 20  
The question: Why do they butt heads?  
The answer: Their big, heavy horns are made for it, and the head-butting shows which one will be dominant, like two bumper cars ramming.

**Creature: Aardwolf**  
Page: 23  
The question: Is this hyena-type animal as mean as it looks?  
The answer: No. It specializes in eating insects, with specialized teeth like flattened pegs, not in eating people or other animals.

**Creature: Horse**  
Page: 24  
The question: Can a horse fight?  
The answer: Horses don't box, but they can raise both back feet at once for a double-whammy kick that can break an adversary's bones.

**Creature: Kitten**  
Page: 25  
The question: What color eyes does a kitten have?  
The answer: All kittens are born with blue eyes. After about 6 weeks the color may change to what their breed normally has.

**Creature: Agouta (Solenodon)**  
Page: 29  
The question: Which small mammal is venomous?  
The answer: It looks like a large shrew with a long whiskered snout. It has a poisonous bite and a venom that paralyzes small prey, but may only cause a human burning or swelling.

**Creature: Squirrel**  
Page: 46  
The question: Why don't squirrels die when they fall out of a tree?  
The answer: They're light and fluffy, with stretchy bodies and bushy tails that help them not get hurt.  
Page: 49  
The question: How do squirrels help burned forests?  
The answer: They leave some of their buried seeds in the ground, and new trees grow from them.  
Page: 100 (Arctic ground squirrel)  
The question: Are there squirrels in the Arctic?  
The answer: Yes. The arctic ground squirrel hibernates up to 8 months and looks like other ground squirrels but is fatter and a bit shorter.

**Creature: Sloth**  
Page: 52  
The question: Who can hold their breath longer, a dolphin or a sloth?  
The answer: The slow-moving sloth. Dolphins need a breath about every 10 minutes, but sloths can hold theirs up to 40 minutes.

**Creature: Camel**  
Page: 60  
The question: How does a male camel attract a mate?  
The answer: It inflates a sac in its mouth called a "dulla," slobbers on it and groans out loud. The page says it's the only animal with this secret weapon.

**Creature: Reindeer**  
Page: 68  
The question: How does a herd protect its weakest members?  
The answer: When threatened, they gather the young and weak in the center and stampede in circles around them. This is called a "reindeer cyclone."

**Creature: Brown hare**  
Page: 83  
The question: Do rabbits box?  
The answer: Brown hares stand tall on their back feet, raise their front feet under their chin and start boxing. Both males and females do it.

**Creature: Platypus**  
Page: 89  
The question: What organ is a platypus missing?  
The answer: It has no stomach. The page lists the duck-like bill, webbed feet, venomous ankle spurs and flat tail first, then adds this oddity.

**Creature: Pig**  
Page: 7  
The question: How did pigs get to an island?  
The answer: About 20 pigs live on an island where pigs aren't native. It appears they swam there, and the page says you can swim in the ocean with them. The island isn't named on the page.

## 🪽 Air and land: birds

**Creature: Red-tailed hawk**  
Page: 6  
The question: Why does a hawk's scream sound so familiar?  
The answer: Its piercing scream is often used in movies to represent any bird of prey, even when the bird on screen isn't a red-tailed hawk.

**Creature: African oxpecker**  
Page: 9  
The question: Why is it called an oxpecker?  
The answer: It rides on large animals like giraffes, rhinos and buffalo and eats the ticks, flies and pests that bother them. The big animals are happy to give it a ride.

**Creature: Ostrich**  
Page: 21  
The question: What can an ostrich do?  
The answer: It's the world's largest bird, lays what may be the largest eggs, and has a kick powerful enough to kill a lion.

**Creature: Eagle**  
Page: 27  
The question: How strong are eagles?  
The answer: Several varieties are among the strongest predators. Their legs and talons are like curved knives, the hind talons are the size of a bear's claws, and they can carry animals that weigh almost as much as they do, such as porcupines, wallabies and monkeys.

**Creature: Black wheatear**  
Page: 28  
The question: How strong is a small bird?  
The answer: To attract a mate, it carries stones weighing up to 4 pounds to decorate the outside of its nest, which is close to its own weight, and it has to fly carrying them.

**Creature: Desertas petrel**  
Page: 30  
The question: Where does it nest, and where does it feed?  
The answer: It nests on the sides of tall cliffs, not in trees, and chases storms at sea (cyclones) to find food, flying as much as 7,500 miles. Cyclones push sea creatures closer to the surface.

**Creature: Bearded vulture**  
Page: 33  
The question: What does it eat and how does it get red?  
The answer: Almost its entire diet is bones. It has been seen dropping a turtle on rocks to crack it open. It dyes its feathers by bathing in iron-rich mud, and the redder the bird, the higher its dominance.

**Creature: Penguin**  
Page: 37  
The question: Why does penguin poop help clouds form?  
The answer: It contains ammonia, which helps seed clouds. Scientists have measured it boosting particle formation for cloud seeding by up to 10,000 times what's in the rest of the area.  
Page: 61 (Emperor penguin)  
The question: How does an emperor penguin chick learn to swim?  
The answer: At just 2 months old it takes its first swim by jumping off an ice cliff that may be as high as 50 feet.

**Creature: Shoebill stork**  
Page: 40  
The question: Why is it also called the whale-headed stork?  
The answer: It has a great big shoe-shaped bill and almost looks prehistoric. It's usually alone and quiet, and when it does speak it sounds like a cow's whisper.

**Creature: Woodpecker**  
Page: 51  
The question: How does it keep from getting a concussion?  
The answer: A woodpecker can peck up to 18,000 times a day, so it wraps its tongue around its brain to prevent a head injury.

**Creature: Great potoo**  
Page: 59  
The question: Why is it so hard to find?  
The answer: It can stand right in front of a tree, and its camouflage blends seamlessly with the tree, so it might win at hide-and-seek.

**Creature: Roller pigeon**  
Page: 67  
The question: Which pigeons do somersaults?  
The answer: Roller pigeons can do backward somersaults while flying, and a second breed does somersaults on the ground.

**Creature: Chicken**  
Page: 54  
The question: What do chickens and Tyrannosaurus rex have in common?  
The answer: They're related. Scientists' analysis found that ostriches and chickens are well-related to the dinosaur.  
Page: 97 (Headless chicken)  
The question: Can a chicken live without a head?  
The answer: One lived for 1½ years after having its head cut off. The farmer missed its jugular vein, so he fed it with an eyedropper until it died.

**Creature: Duck**  
Page: 69  
The question: Do ducks eat other ducks?  
The answer: It's unusual but it happens, when they are over-populated, bored, have nutritional needs, or have poor lighting and ventilation.

**Creature: Crow**  
Page: 73  
The question: Why are crows linked to death?  
The answer: They followed armies at war, knowing they could feed on dead bodies. They are both carrion birds and intelligent, so they learned that war leaves bodies.

## 🦇 Air: bats

**Creature: Mexican free-tailed bat**  
Page: 70  
The question: Do bats sing?  
The answer: Yes. It sings love songs of chirps, buzzes and trills to attract a mate, using the same "words" in its phrases. People rarely hear them because the songs are too high for us.

## 🐞 Insects and bugs

**Creature: Bed bug**  
Page: 14  
The question: Why are bed bugs so hard to find?  
The answer: They're only as wide as the thin side of a credit card and they come out at night while we sleep, feeding for just 5–10 minutes and returning to their hiding place.

**Creature: Hair worm (in a cricket)**  
Page: 15  
The question: How does a worm control a cricket?  
The answer: The worm rides inside a cricket and feasts on its fat until it's full, then takes over the cricket's mind and makes it head to water. It doesn't bother people.

**Creature: Butterfly**  
Page: 31  
The question: Can butterflies make electricity?  
The answer: Flapping wings can build static electricity, so pollen comes flying to them and they don't even need to touch the flowers to pollinate them.  
Page: 91  
The question: Why don't butterflies fly in a straight line?  
The answer: A straight line would get a defenseless butterfly eaten, so they flit up, left, down and right to confuse birds. If they are caught, they taste yucky.  
Page: 96 (Caterpillar)  
The question: What's inside a chrysalis?  
The answer: A soup. The caterpillar must "digest" itself into goo to become a butterfly, and cutting it open would splash you.

**Creature: Cicada**  
Page: 62  
The question: What is the cicada's "potty superpower"?  
The answer: One variety stays in the earth 13–17 years, then comes out all at once. Females call with hums and clicks, and a cicada can shoot a stream of urine at up to 3 meters per second, faster than any other animal observed. Mosquitoes, aphids and flies have just a drip.

**Creature: Bee**  
Page: 45 (Stingless bees)  
The question: How do bees that can't sting protect their hives?  
The answer: Each night they cover the hive entrance with a barrier like a fence to keep out ants and others. If you try to steal their honey, they may harass you by tangling in your hair or even your eyebrow.  
Page: 75 (Bumblebees)  
The question: Can bees teach each other?  
The answer: Yes. One study taught a bee a puzzle, and that bee taught another bee to do the same thing.

**Creature: Mosquito**  
Page: 48  
The question: Why do mosquito bites itch?  
The answer: The itch isn't from the blood-sucking but from the saliva the female injects before and after biting, which keeps your blood from clotting so she can drink more.

**Creature: Matabele ants**  
Page: 80  
The question: Do ants help their wounded?  
The answer: Yes. These termite hunters carry their wounded back home to be looked after and can make a salve with healing ingredients.

**Creature: Robber fly**  
Page: 98  
The question: Which fly eats other flies?  
The answer: The robber fly may be the biggest fly you'll ever see, about 3 inches long, and it injects a fluid that breaks down muscle and paralyzes its victims.

## 🕷️ Spiders and other crawlers

**Creature: Demon-horned orb weaver**  
Page: 63  
The question: Why does it have horns?  
The answer: It makes very intricate wheel-shaped webs, and its horns make it look much bigger, hopefully scaring off those trying to eat it.

**Creature: Darwin's bark spider**  
Page: 64  
The question: How far can it shoot its web?  
The answer: As much as 25 meters (over 75 feet). The female is larger than the male, and it makes some of the toughest web fiber known.

**Creature: Swimming spider**  
Page: 74  
The question: Can a spider swim?  
The answer: Yes. They live near water, walk on top of it, and dive and swim under the surface to find prey or escape being prey.

**Creature: Joro spider**  
Page: 88  
The question: Is a big colorful spider dangerous?  
The answer: Females are bright yellow and blue striped with long black and yellow legs, and make webs up to 6 feet long. It eats insects you don't want in your home and its venom paralyzes prey but is not considered dangerous to you.

**Creature: Leech**  
Page: 47  
The question: What's the secret about leeches?  
The answer: Doctors and hospitals use them to help people with blood problems, and they have 32 brains.

## 🐊 Reptiles and amphibians

**Creature: Frog**  
Page: 4 (Orange frog, *Dyscophus guineti*)  
The question: How does a little orange frog defend itself?  
The answer: It shoots out "glue" toward the attacker's face and glues its mouth shut.  
Page: 19  
The question: How do frogs breathe without lungs?  
The answer: Many species have no lungs and breathe through their skin, which absorbs oxygen.  
Page: 42 (Magnificent tree frog, *Litoria splendida*)  
The question: What's rare about this frog?  
The answer: Researchers in Australia found a very rare blue-skinned one, never seen before. The scientific name means "splendid" or "magnificent."

**Creature: Turtle**  
Page: 18  
The question: How do turtles survive winter under the ice?  
The answer: They breathe out of their butt.

**Creature: Legless lizard**  
Page: 32  
The question: How can you tell a legless lizard from a snake?  
The answer: Snakes have no eyelids or ear openings, lizards do. Also, the legless lizard has a long tail and short body, and a snake has a long body and short tail.

**Creature: Snake**  
Page: 34 (Snakes in general)  
The question: Poisonous or venomous?  
The answer: Most snakes are venomous, not poisonous. Poison is put ON you and venom is put IN you.  
Page: 35 (Poisonous snakes)  
The question: Which snakes are really poisonous?  
The answer: As far as the author knows only two. They eat things with a toxin and ooze it through their skin, so touching them can poison you.  
Page: 36 (Barbados threadsnake)  
The question: Which snake is the smallest?  
The answer: It grows only about 5 inches long, too small for teeth like other snakes, so it eats very small things like termites.  
Page: 38  
The question: Why do snakes go into a "food coma"?  
The answer: Snakes that don't need to eat often eat big, and after a big meal they go into a food coma to digest it, like after a Thanksgiving meal.  
Page: 39  
The question: How does a snake fit a big meal?  
The answer: It has no diaphragm, so its heart has room to move around, and it can move its heart to make room.  
Page: 58 (Spider-tailed horned viper)  
The question: How does it catch birds?  
The answer: It has a tail that looks like a spider and uses it to attract birds, then captures the bird.

## 🌊 Sea mammals and water animals

**Creature: Seal**  
Page: 10  
The question: Why does a seal do the "banana pose"?  
The answer: It only does it when feeling safe and comfortable. It also helps regulate body temperature and keeps the extremities dry.

**Creature: Manatee**  
Page: 17  
The question: How does a manatee float or sink?  
The answer: It has no swim bladder. It uses a supply of its own gas: it holds its "fart" to float and lets it out to sink.

**Creature: Humpback whale**  
Page: 41  
The question: Do whales protect other animals?  
The answer: Over 115 cases have been recorded of humpbacks protecting smaller fishes (and seals) from orcas, like bodyguards.

**Creature: Blue whale**  
Page: 53  
The question: How slow can a heart beat?  
The answer: A blue whale can slow its heart until it beats only twice a minute. A person's is about 60–100 a minute. The page says the sloth also slows its heart rate to hold its breath.

**Creature: Orca**  
Page: 95  
The question: How does an orca attack a great white shark?  
The answer: It attacks the shark's underbelly and with great precision bites and pulls out the liver, about 30% of the shark's body, which will keep an orca full for a day.

**Creature: Sea otter**  
Page: 101  
The question: How dense is sea otter fur?  
The answer: The densest of any animal, 850,000 to 1 million hairs in a 1-inch patch. Two layers: outer "guard" fur keeps the under layer dry and traps air next to the skin to stay warm.

## 🐟 Fish and sharks

**Creature: Garden eel**  
Page: 11  
The question: How does a garden eel find food?  
The answer: It stays sticking up out of a hole its whole life and waits for food to pass, looking like wheat blowing in the current.

**Creature: Requiem shark**  
Page: 22  
The question: How many teeth does a shark use?  
The answer: It can grow and lose as many as 50,000 teeth in its lifetime.

**Creature: Piranha**  
Page: 43  
The question: Do fish make sounds?  
The answer: Yes, piranhas can bark, by squeezing their swim bladder into different shapes, and each species makes a different sound. Other fish can scream when bitten.

**Creature: Whitemargin stargazer**  
Page: 44  
The question: Why is it hard to see?  
The answer: It buries itself in the sand with only its eyes sticking out.

**Creature: Fangtooth fish**  
Page: 66  
The question: How does it close its mouth without stabbing itself?  
The answer: It has special "pockets" in its upper jaw for the teeth to fit, like a tooth "garage."

**Creature: Pelican eel**  
Page: 65  
The question: Why is it named for a pelican?  
The answer: Its big mouth works like a pelican's pouch, holding lots of fish, and it has a bioluminescent light at its tail tip to lure prey.

**Creature: Chaunacops fish**  
Page: 81  
The question: How does it catch prey?  
The answer: It's round with big feet-like fins and is usually red, pink or orange. Like an army sniper it stays motionless and blends in, then ambushes prey. It's not a strong swimmer but "walks" pretty well.

**Creature: Drumming fish**  
Page: 87  
The question: Can a tiny fish be louder than an elephant?  
The answer: Yes. It's under an inch long (the printed fraction is hard to read) and as loud as an airplane taking off. It has a "drumming cartilage," a special rib, and a muscle that doesn't get tired, and hits the cartilage against its swim bladder.  
Page: 90  
The question: How does the loud fish do it?  
The answer: Recap of page 87: special muscles beat against its swim bladder, and the noise is used for communication.

**Creature: Parasite louse (tongue-eating louse)**  
Page: 72  
The question: Can a parasite replace a fish's tongue?  
The answer: A parasite enters the fish's mouth, eats the tongue and replaces itself as the new tongue. The fish keeps living and uses the louse as its tongue. It isn't harmful to humans.

**Creature: Lantern shark**  
Page: 93  
The question: Are there new animals still to be found?  
The answer: Yes. Fishermen pulled up an unfamiliar bioluminescent shark with long snouts, large gills and a wide mouth with lots of teeth. Because it can "light up," it was called the "lantern shark."

## 🦀 Sea invertebrates

**Creature: Sand bubbler crab**  
Page: 1  
The question: How does a small beach crab eat?  
The answer: It rolls sand around in its mouth to eat the plankton in the sand, then spits out a ball of sand. The incoming tide "melts" the sand back into the beach.

**Creature: Octopus**  
Page: 16  
The question: How smart is an octopus?  
The answer: It can unscrew lids, work out mazes and learn caretakers' schedules so it can sneak out of its aquarium and steal fish from others.  
Page: 76 (Octopus and squid)  
The question: How many hearts do they have?  
The answer: 3. One pumps blood through the body and the other two are for the gills.  
Page: 94  
The question: Do octopuses punch fish?  
The answer: Yes. They might punch a fish for not acting right, or for no known reason.

**Creature: Wunderpus**  
Page: 77  
The question: How does it disguise itself?  
The answer: It's an octopus-like creature with long thin arms, white stripes and polka dots. It can change color, movement and shape to mimic other animals. It also has two eyes on tall stalks.

**Creature: Cuttlefish**  
Page: 78  
The question: How does it flirt?  
The answer: Special skin cells let it change color instantly. When courting a female it disguises its other side as a female so rival males don't get jealous.

**Creature: Sandworm**  
Page: 79  
The question: Are sandworms only bait?  
The answer: No, they're serious predators. Some build tubes or burrows out of mucus, some change color, and some can eat an octopus. They have teeth and a big jaw.

**Creature: Squat lobster**  
Page: 82  
The question: How does it warn predators?  
The answer: It looks different from other lobsters, with long extended claws and a brightly colored body. When threatened it moves backward and sideways quickly and shows off the claws.

**Creature: Whiplash squid**  
Page: 84  
The question: How does it hide?  
The answer: Its body is almost transparent and it has bioluminescent organs that match the sunlight so predators can't find it. Its two tentacles are long and whip-like.

**Creature: Oblong urchin (Dermechinus)**  
Page: 85  
The question: What makes it unusual?  
The answer: Most urchins are round, but this one is oblong, and its spines are shorter than most, but still protect it.

**Creature: Crown jellyfish**  
Page: 86  
The question: Why does it light up?  
The answer: When the bell is touched it lights up, and otherwise it's transparent. When attacked, its lights scare and distract a predator, and it also uses the light to attract prey.

**Creature: Cone snail**  
Page: 71  
The question: Why is it dangerous?  
The answer: It's venomous, with a special harpoon-like tooth and a gland that delivers venom to paralyze prey. Named for its cone-shaped, colorful shell, it looks like an ice-cream cone on its side.

**Creature: Starfish**  
Page: 55  
The question: Where are a starfish's eyes?  
The answer: A starfish has 5 arms with one eye at the end of each.  
Page: 56  
The question: What happens if it loses an arm?  
The answer: It grows back, including the eye.

**Creature: Snail**  
Page: 92  
The question: How long can a snail sleep?  
The answer: Snails can go to sleep for up to 3 years.

**Creature: Plankton**  
Page: 50  
The question: How much oxygen does plankton make?  
The answer: As many as 20,000 fit in one drop of water. The page says plankton create about 80% of Earth's oxygen, and 1 out of every 5 breaths you take.

**Creature: Komodo dragon**  
Page: 57  
The question: How does it kill?  
The answer: It bites and injects venom, then follows the prey until it can't go any further, and then eats it. The page continues an earlier book's Komodo page.

## What the book shows overall

- **This book is more about "survival and weird abilities" than a theme.** Pages group loosely into boxing (kangaroo, horse, hare), breathing oddities (turtle, frog, sloth, whale), snakes (pages 34–39), deep-sea light and disguise (pages 65, 77, 78, 84, 86, 93), and spiders (pages 63, 64, 74, 88, 99).
- **Three hunting-by-disguise runs.** Pages 58–59 (viper, potoo), 77–78 (wunderpus, cuttlefish) and 81 (Chaunacops).
- **The caption jokes lean on cartoons and costumes.** Examples are the oxpecker saying "Please, just call me Bob!", the spider in a Spider-Man mask, the black wheatear's "She better love this nest," and the manatee "submarine."
- **Safari Sam appears as a character** (the explorer in the pith helmet) on many pages, often as the one being addressed by the animal.
- **It refers back to earlier books** in the series, for example the woodpecker (p51), the platypus (p89) and the Komodo dragon (p57).

## Things you may want to check

1. **Repeats.** Pages 87 and 90 are about the same noisy fish. Page 50's plankton fact also appears in the Cosmos book (page 10).
2. **Facts that look wrong or unverified.**
   - Page 50 says plankton make about 80% of Earth's oxygen but also says 1 in 5 breaths. Those two numbers don't match, and scientists usually give 50–80%.
   - Page 31's claim that butterflies make so much static electricity that pollen jumps to them is usually said of bees.
   - Page 61 says emperor penguin chicks are "just 2 months" old when they jump off cliffs. Chicks fledge at around 5 months.
   - Page 68's "reindeer cyclone" is not a widely documented behavior.
   - Page 73's claim that crows followed armies to war is folklore.
   - Page 35 says there are "only two" poisonous snakes. The page itself says "as far as I know," and more are known.
   - Page 27's claim that eagles can carry animals nearly as heavy as themselves holds for the largest eagles only.
   - Page 21's "kick can kill a lion" is commonly repeated but anecdotal.
   - Page 62's cicada urine jet (3 m/s) matches a published study, but "faster than any other animal observed, including humans, elephants and horses" is the book's own comparison and I haven't checked it.
3. **Unnamed animals.** Page 7 doesn't name the pig island (it's in the Bahamas). Page 35 doesn't name the two poisonous snakes. Page 87 doesn't name the tiny fish.
4. **Typos I noticed.**
   - "pollenate" (p31)
   - "breath" for "breathe" (p19)
   - "coupe weeks" (p38)
   - "the have a venom" (p29)
   - "are is larger" (p64)
   - "Antartica" (p37)
   - "cartilidge" (p87)
   - "Ardwolf" for "Aardwolf" (p23)
5. **OCR limits.** About 60 pages had scrambled text, and I confirmed them against the page images. I read all the cartoon captions I could see on the images, including the ones the OCR dropped.
