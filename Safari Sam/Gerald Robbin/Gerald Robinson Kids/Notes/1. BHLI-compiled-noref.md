# Because He Liked It: all creatures, objects and topics compiled (no references)

This file combines the notes from eight *Because He Liked It!* books into one list, without book or page references. Animals are grouped by category, with the same creature from different books together under one heading. Plants (from the Plants book) and Space and Earth (from the Cosmos book) keep the groupings of their own books.

# PART 1: ANIMALS

## 🐾 Land: pets and farm animals (9)

**Creature: Ankole-Watusi (cattle)**  
The question: What is it famous for?  
The answer: The world's largest horns by circumference.

**Creature: Cat**  
The question: Can cats see in the dark?  
The answer: Not total dark, but in low light they see 6–8× better than we do.  
The question: How expressive is a cat?  
The answer: 276 different facial expressions, against about 44 facial movements for humans.  
The question: Why do cats squeeze through tiny gaps?  
The answer: They judge their body size better than dogs do, so they go through quickly.  
The question: How do cats land on their feet?  
The answer: They have 40 more bones than humans and a very flexible spine, so they can spin in the air.  
The question: How do cats walk differently?  
The answer: People and most animals walk on heels and toes, but cats walk mainly on their toes.  
The question: Is there a cat that likes water?  
The answer: Yes. The Van cat loves to swim and is nicknamed the "swimming cat." The breed began near Van Lake in Turkey. Its eyes are born blue and change to amber or darker blue, and some have one of each.  
The question: What color eyes does a kitten have?  
The answer: All kittens are born with blue eyes. After about 6 weeks the color may change to what their breed normally has.  
The question: What's odd about a cat's urine?  
The answer: It glows under a blacklight.  
The question: Why are cats scared of cucumbers?  
The answer: They get startled when anything suddenly appears, especially cucumbers, which the page guesses they mistake for snakes.  
The question: How many toes does a cat have?  
The answer: 5 on each front foot but only 4 on each back foot.  
The question: Why don't cats "talk" to each other?  
The answer: Dogs bark at each other, people and anything that moves, but cats only meow to get a human's attention and don't meow at each other.  
The question: Can cats taste sugar?  
The answer: No. A recent study found cats don't have a sweet tooth.  
The question: Can a cat be allergic to people?  
The answer: Some cats show signs of allergies near human dandruff.

**Creature: Chicken**  
The question: What do chickens and Tyrannosaurus rex have in common?  
The answer: They're related. Scientists' analysis found that ostriches and chickens are well-related to the dinosaur.  
The question: Can a chicken live without a head?  
The answer: One lived for 1½ years after having its head cut off. The farmer missed its jugular vein, so he fed it with an eyedropper until it died.  
The question: Can chickens say more than "cluck"?  
The answer: Yes. They have about 14 different clucking sounds that warn of danger, food and more, and can tell the difference between about 30 different birds, numbers, shapes, patterns and even human faces.  
The question: What do chickens and *Tyrannosaurus rex* have in common?  
The answer: They're related. A molecular analysis found that ostriches and chickens are well-related to the dinosaur.

**Creature: Cow**  
The question: Why no upper front teeth?  
The answer: It pulls grass with its bottom teeth and has molars at the back, so it doesn't need them.  
The question: Do cows speak different languages?  
The answer: Cows from different places differ in the volume, tone and sound of their moos.  
The question: How well can a cow see?  
The answer: Its eyes are on the sides of its face, so it can see almost all the way around, except directly behind. It has poor depth perception, so it's nearly impossible to get a cow to walk down stairs.

**Creature: Dog**  
The question: How good is a dog's nose?  
The answer: 1,000× better than ours. It can follow trails 12 days old, and the nose's findings count as evidence in court.  
The question: How does it know it's walk time?  
The answer: It gets sensitive to routine, so a regular schedule teaches it the time.  
The question: Which nostril does it use?  
The answer: It sniffs nice smells with the left nostril and keeps the right for danger.  
The question: Can a dog watch over your health?  
The answer: It can detect dangerous blood sugar, sense a coming seizure, and smell some cancers.  
The question: What else is special about dogs?  
The answer: They understand human emotions, have unique "nose prints," and can sing in harmony.  
The question: Why do puppies wrestle, and who wins?  
The answer: Play-wrestling teaches them skills they need. Sometimes the male pups let the female pups win even when the males are stronger.  
The question: Did dogs descend from wolves?  
The answer: The page says many people believe so, but wolves don't have the muscles to make facial expressions like a dog's sad or delighted eyebrow movements.  
The question: Are dalmatians born spotted?  
The answer: Most are born white. The spots show as they grow, and every dalmatian's spots are unique, like zebra stripes.  
The question: How good is a dog's nose?  
The answer: Dogs can sniff out drugs, explosives and people, because the smell area in their brain is 40 times bigger than ours.  
The question: How good is a dog's hearing?  
The answer: They hear a dog whistle people can't, and special ear muscles let them tune in sounds and move their ears in many directions.  
The question: Can a dog tell when you're sad?  
The answer: Dogs can tell when you're sick, and when you're heart-broken, sad or depressed, and they comfort you by cuddling.  
The question: Why do dog feet smell like corn chips?  
The answer: Dogs sweat through their paws, and sweat and bacteria clogged in the paws can make them smell like corn, corn chips or popcorn ("Frito feet").

**Creature: Goat**  
The question: Can goats read faces?  
The answer: Yes. Researchers showed goats side-by-side pictures of people, one happy and one sad, and the goats gave far more attention to the happy face.  
The question: Which goat climbs trees?  
The answer: A goat that loves Argan berries, which grow on trees, so it learned to climb the trees to get its favorite food.  
The question: Can goats read faces?  
The answer: They can tell if someone is happy or sad and spend more time with those who have the happy face.

**Creature: Horse**  
The question: Can a horse fight?  
The answer: Horses don't box, but they can raise both back feet at once for a double-whammy kick that can break an adversary's bones.  
The question: Can a horse breathe through its mouth?  
The answer: No. Even after a hard race, horses can only breathe through their nostrils.  
The question: Who are a horse's cousins?  
The answer: Horses are distantly related to rhinoceroses and tapirs, though none of the three look alike.

**Creature: Lamb**  
The question: What is a "bummer lamb"?  
The answer: If a mother sheep rejects her newborn, she never changes her mind. The lamb acts depressed and hangs its head low, and a rancher or farmer will adopt it and raise it until it can rejoin the sheep.

**Creature: Silkie chicken**  
The question: What's odd about it?  
The answer: It's fluffy and friendly like a pet, with white fur-like feathers over black or dark-blue skin.

The question: Which small mammal is venomous?  
The answer: It looks like a large shrew with a long whiskered snout. It has a poisonous bite and a venom that paralyzes small prey, but may only cause a human burning or swelling.

**Creature: Anteater**  
The question: How do anteaters eat?  
The answer: A long slender nose pokes into an ant village and its tongue stretches up to 24 inches, flicking 150 times a minute, enough to eat 30,000 ants a day. The page begins by comparing aardvarks and anteaters, but only describes the anteater.  
The question: How does an anteater eat without teeth?  
The answer: Its long sticky tongue gathers the ants, and its stomach muscles crush them once swallowed.

**Creature: Armadillo**  
The question: How tough is its armor?  
The answer: Almost bullet-proof. A bullet bounced off one and hit the man who shot it.  
The question: How does an armadillo protect itself?  
The answer: It has an armor-like shell and curls into a ball with only its armor exposed. When the danger is over, it springs back onto all four legs.  
The question: Which armadillo looks like a bunny?  
The answer: It looks like a cuddly bunny without ears, wearing an armor coat. It comes out at night to eat plants, worms and bugs.

**Creature: Aye-aye**  
The question: How does it find grubs inside wood?  
The answer: It taps trees with one long, bony finger, listens for the echo of grubs, eats a hole in the wood and hooks the grubs out.

The question: What's strange about its tusks?  
The answer: It looks like a wild pig and it is one. Its two tusks begin in the mouth like teeth and then grow right through the skin and come up in the face.  
The question: What's odd about its tusks?  
The answer: It's also known as a deer pig. Its tusks extend from the mouth up through the snout and point up over the eyes toward the head.

**Creature: Bear**

The question: How smart are bears?  
The answer: Big brains: they hide from predators, cover their tracks, solve complex tasks, and may remember creatures they met years earlier.  
The question: Should you play dead if a bear finds you?  
The answer: The page says the latest advice is to run, because a bear's bite can crush a bowling ball.

The answer: When it's a bison. Bison have a thick coat, a much larger head with a small beard and big bulging shoulders, and small pointy horns, where buffalo have much longer ones. The American bison was mistakenly called a buffalo.

The question: Do buffalo vote?  
The answer: Yes, only the adult females. When it's time to travel, they stand, look in one direction and lie back down, which "votes" on the way to go.

**Creature: Elephant**  
The question: Why does it suck its trunk?  
The answer: For comfort, like a baby's thumb. The trunk has about 40,000 muscles.  
The question: What scares a giant?  
The answer: Bees, which sting the mouth and eyes. Farmers keep bees to keep elephants away.  
The question: Do elephants really remember?  
The answer: Yes. One recognized its zookeeper's scent after 13 years.  
The question: How do elephants talk over long distances?  
The answer: In sounds too low for people to hear, sent through their feet into the ground. Other elephants feel the vibrations.  
The question: Can an elephant jump?  
The answer: No. It's the only land mammal unable to jump.  
The question: Can elephants swim?  
The answer: Yes, they're great swimmers. They use the trunk like a snorkel and breathe through it with the mouth underwater.  
The question: Is a tusk a horn or an antler?  
The answer: Neither. It's made of the same material as a tooth.  
The question: Where do some elephants go at night?  
The answer: To an underground cave (the page calls it the "elephant basement") that is cool and safe, with salty walls they lick for calcium, sodium and magnesium.  
The question: How is elephant skin different from ours?  
The answer: It doesn't flake off. Older skin gets harder and builds up in layers, so elephants need water poured on them to soak in. Their only sweat glands are between their toes, so they also use their ears as fans.  
The question: Do elephants mourn?  
The answer: They show emotions for each other. When an elephant dies, others may try to bury it and come back to that spot long after, as if showing respect or mourning.  
The question: How strong is an elephant's trunk?  
The answer: It uses its trunk like arms to pick up things, feel things and eat, and the trunk has over 40,000 muscles.  
The question: Can elephants swim?  
The answer: Yes, they are great swimmers and use their trunk as a snorkel to swim under the water. They can swim 30 miles in a day.  
The question: Do baby elephants suck their trunks?  
The answer: Yes, like a baby sucking a thumb, for comfort when stressed.

**Creature: Elephant shrew**  
The question: Why that name?

**Creature: Fossa**  
The question: What is a fossa?  
The answer: A mix: a cat's head and claws, a mongoose's long body, a monkey's tail, small round ears, and a skunk-like stink gland.

**Creature: Fox**  
The question: Is a fox like other dogs?  
The answer: No. It's most active at night, pounces like a cat, uses whiskers like a cat, has spines on its tongue, and some have retractable claws to climb trees and sleep in branches.  
The question: How cold can an arctic fox stand?  
The answer: Its fur is so thick it doesn't shiver until −94°F (−70°C), and then it wraps its tail around itself like a blanket.

**Creature: Gerenuk**

**Creature: Giant panda**  
The question: How big is a newborn?  
The answer: About the size of a stick of butter.  
The question: How does a panda hold bamboo?  
The answer: It has an elongated wrist-bone that sticks out and uses it like a thumb to hold the bamboo shoot.

**Creature: Giraffe**  
The question: What's hidden about it?  
The answer: Its resting heart rate is like a person's, but it has the highest blood pressure of any animal.  
The question: How does its heart work with such a long neck?  
The answer: Its heart is the size of a basketball. When it drinks, it spreads its legs to lower the heart level so blood won't rush out of its brain. A special pad at the base of the brain keeps blood supplied so it doesn't get dizzy.

**Creature: Gorilla**  
The question: How strong is a silverback gorilla?  
The answer: 10–20 times stronger than people, with a bite stronger than a tiger's but not a whale's. The page has a "Bite-O-Meter" chart.

**Creature: Honey badger**  
The question: How fearless is it?  
The answer: It kills snakes by chewing their heads in two and keeps going after honey despite bee stings. Some have been found stung to death inside the hive.

**Creature: Hyena**  
The question: How smart is it?  
The answer: About as smart as a primate. They live and hunt in groups, have very powerful jaws, and "talk" with laughs, growls and whoops.

**Creature: Kangaroo**  
The question: Do kangaroos really box?  
The answer: Yes, boxing seems to be part of their nature. Years ago a sideshow might have a man box a kangaroo, and since kangaroos can also use their very powerful hind feet, the match could be vicious.  
The question: How big is a newborn kangaroo?  
The answer: About the size of a jellybean (an inch long), blind, deaf and bald. It spends the next six months in the pouch. Most kangaroos are left-handed.

**Creature: Kinkajou**  
The question: How does it move through the trees?  
The answer: It has a long tongue and a very strong tail. It can climb up it or hang down from it.

**Creature: Koala**  
The question: Are koalas smart?  
The answer: No. A koala's brain is small and smooth, and if you put its eucalyptus leaf on a napkin or plate, it wouldn't know what to do, since as far as it knows the meal is still on the plant. They don't seem to know what rain is.

**Creature: Leopard**  
The question: How big is a leopard?  
The answer: About 2 feet tall at the shoulder but up to 7 feet long, and up to 125 pounds. It stays away from you unless made anxious or afraid.

**Creature: Lion**  
The question: How can you tell a lion's age?  
The answer: By its nose. After 3 months it turns pink to light gray, then gets freckles and then liver spots, and at about age 8 it turns black.

**Creature: Llama**  
The question: What does a llama do when it's mad?  
The answer: It spits, even on another llama. A llama covered in another's half-eaten grass won't eat the rest of the day.

**Creature: Mouse**

**Creature: Okapi**  
The question: What's odd about its tongue?  
The answer: It looks a little like a deer, a llama and a zebra, and its tongue is long enough to wash its eyelids and clean its ears.

**Creature: Opossum**  
The question: What's its superpower?  
The answer: It eats up to 4,000 ticks a week. Its "playing dead" is really fainting, and it resists rabies and rattlesnake venom.  
The question: Can an animal survive snake bites?  
The answer: It can withstand about 80 bites from a rattlesnake or coral snake without being affected. Studying it has helped make anti-venom.  
The question: Why do cars kill more opossums than snakes?  
The answer: A peptide in the opossum neutralizes snake venom, so it suffers no ill effects. The page says honey badgers and squirrels have a similar ability.  
The question: How does a newborn opossum feed?  
The answer: It's about the size of a honeybee and doesn't know how to suck, so the mother tightens her muscles to push, squirt or pump milk into the baby.  
The question: Are opossums clean?  
The answer: Yes. They bathe often and clean up when eating, even though they eat rotting flesh.

**Creature: Pangolin**  
The question: Is a pangolin an armadillo?  
The answer: It reminds the author of an armadillo, covered in armor made of keratin, like your fingernails, and it can curl into a ball. It is hunted for its meat, and its scales are used in medicines.

The answer: It's like a large rabbit but more related to a rodent. It has a kangaroo-like face and a dog or rabbit body, with short front legs for digging and long back legs for jumping. Hand-raised ones make wonderful pets and are easy to walk on a leash.

**Creature: Rat**  
The question: Can a rat sniff out smuggled goods?  
The answer: These rats grow to almost 3 feet long, and are trained to sniff out rhino horns, pangolin scales and elephant tusks in warehouses and shipyards.  
The question: Why were rats a plague?  
The answer: A pair can produce as many as 2,000 babies in a year, shed over a million hairs and drop about 25,000 poops a year. They squeeze through a very small hole, swim a long way, and hold their breath for 3 minutes. (The page gives the hole width and swim distance as fractions the OCR dropped, "an inch" and "mile," so I left out the numbers.)  
The question: How athletic is a rat?  
The answer: It can fall 50 feet without getting hurt, jump 3 feet high and leap 48 inches.

The answer: Scientists don't yet know whether all galaxy clusters are affected. The theory is that something we can't see is pulling on everything we see.

**Creature: Hole in the universe**  
The question: Is there really a hole in the universe?  
The answer: A satellite mapping leftover heat found a cold spot where searches turn up no matter at all. Scientists have no explanation.

**Creature: Light-year**  
The question: How do we measure space?  
The answer: An AU (about 93 million miles) is used inside the solar system, and a light-year is used beyond it. A light-year is almost 6 trillion miles, about 95,000 years in one of the fastest jets.  
The question: Could you ever get there?  
The answer: No. At 95,000 years for one light-year, you, your children and your great-great-great-great-great-grandchildren wouldn't arrive.  
The question: How far is the nearest star?  
The answer: Proxima Centauri is 4.5 light-years away, over 400,000 years by the world's fastest jet. That's why scientists talk in light-years instead of miles.

## ☀️ The Sun and stars (6)

**Creature: Sun**  
The question: Is the sun a ball of fire?  
The answer: No. Fire needs heat, oxygen and something combustible. The sun is glowing gas powered by nuclear fusion.  
The question: Can the sun become a black hole?  
The answer: No, it doesn't have enough mass. It would have to grow about 20 times its size.  
The question: How hot is the sun?  
The answer: The surface is about 10,000°F and the center may reach more than 27 million degrees.  
The question: Why isn't the sun too bright?  
The answer: Earth's atmosphere reflects about 30% of the sunlight back into space.  
The question: How much energy does the sun give Earth?  
The answer: In just 1 hour, Earth receives more energy from the sun than fossil fuels use in an entire year.  
The question: How long does sunlight take to reach us?  
The answer: About 8 minutes. If the sun turned off, we wouldn't know for 8 minutes.  

----

**Creature: Brown dwarfs**  
The question: What are failed stars?  
The answer: Stars called brown dwarfs, some of which are hardly warm enough to bake a potato.  

----

**Creature: Betelgeuse**  
The question: How big is this red supergiant?  
The answer: It's known for its huge size and red color. Scientists recently found two unexplained bright spots on it, as far apart as the sun is from Earth. Placed where our sun is, it would swallow the earth.  

----

**Creature: Brightest star**  
The question: What is the biggest, brightest known star?  
The answer: A star in a galaxy next to the Milky Way, about 265 times the mass of our sun and about 10 million times as bright.  

----

**Creature: Black hole**  
The question: Can the sun become one?  
The answer: No. See "Sun" above.  
The question: Do black holes dance?  
The answer: Like two magnets about to pull together, researchers found 33 pairs of supermassive black holes that have begun or will begin to "dance" at about 500,000 mph, even though they may be 3,000 to 8,000 light-years apart.  
The question: What is a black hole?  
The answer: A region in space that acts like a super-strong vacuum cleaner. A star pulled in spirals like water down a drain and heats to millions of degrees, and its glow is the clue that a black hole is nearby.  
The question: Do gamma rays make a Hulk?  
The answer: No. Gamma-ray bursts are the most powerful explosions in the universe, happening when a massive star collapses. They can destroy almost anything nearby and may cause a black hole.  

----

**Creature: Sound in space**  
The question: What does space sound like?  
The answer: Mostly quiet, since sound can't travel in a vacuum. An exploding star could send out a shock wave (up to 20,000 miles per second) carrying material that sound could travel through, and it would be incredibly loud.

## 🌍 Earth: the planet as a whole (9)

**Creature: Earth (shape, spin and speed)**  
The question: Is the earth ever still?  
The answer: It never stands still. It spins, orbits the sun, and keeps changing shape from earthquakes, volcanoes, shifting plates and the pull of the sun and moon (tides).  
The question: How wide is the earth?  
The answer: The radius at the equator is 4,025 miles.  
The question: Is Earth a perfect ball?  
The answer: No. It bulges at the equator because of its spin.  
The question: Are days getting longer?  
The answer: Yes, by about 1.7 milliseconds each century.  

----

**Creature: Earth's core**  
The question: Where does life live?  
The answer: All known life lives in the top 25 miles of the surface, only about 1% of the earth, because the core is molten metal under huge pressure.  
The question: Is anything on earth hotter than the sun?  
The answer: Yes, the core is 12,600°F and the sun's surface is 10,000°F.  
The question: Why is the core still hot?  
The answer: Not because of an endless fire, but because radioactive isotopes release a steady stream of energy that turns to heat.  

----

**Creature: Earth's magnetic field**  
The question: What does the magnetic field do?  
The answer: It moves west about 10 miles a year and protects us from particles from the sun. Some scientists think the poles may swap, which would expose us to more meteorites and asteroids and confuse migrating animals.  

----

**Creature: Earth's three conditions for life**  
The question: What does a planet need for life?  
The answer: It must be in the "goldilocks" zone (not too far or too close to its sun), the right size, and have the right chemicals like oxygen, carbon dioxide and water.  

----

**Creature: Earth's water**  
The question: Is there water elsewhere in our solar system?  
The answer: Earth is the only planet in our solar system with water on its surface, nearly three-fourths of it.  
The question: How much water can we drink?  
The answer: About 70% of Earth is water, but only 2.5% of that is fresh, and most of that is frozen at the poles.

**Creature: Earth's overpopulation**  
The question: Is Earth overpopulated?

**Creature: Phytoplankton**

**Creature: Gold**

## 🌊 Oceans and ice (4)

**Creature: Ocean**  
The question: Where is Earth's biggest waterfall?  
The answer: Under the ocean between Greenland and Iceland, where cold dense water sinks nearly 2 miles beneath warmer water. It's over 100 miles wide.  
The question: How much of the ocean is mapped?  
The answer: Only 19%, so most of it is unexplored.  
The question: What are blue holes?  
The answer: Places where the ocean floor suddenly drops several hundred feet, turning the water dark blue. They lack oxygen and have little life.  
The question: What is red tide?

The question: How much water moves around the globe?

The question: What drives the currents?

The question: How deep is the deepest place?  
The answer: Nearly 7 miles, deeper than the tallest mountain is tall, with 1,100 times the pressure at sea level.
