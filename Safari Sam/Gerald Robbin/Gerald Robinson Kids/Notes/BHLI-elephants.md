I read all 101 pages of the *Because He Liked It!* book in the file named "elephants interior" (the cover title is printed on top of itself, so the OCR can't read it, and despite the file name only two pages are about elephants). The text layer was mostly clean, and I checked a handful of pages against the page images. Each page follows the usual pattern: a "Why did God do that?" header, a short fact about an animal, and a joke caption from the animal in a speech bubble. The answer is always "Because He liked it!"

The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Creatures that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives.

## 🐾 Land: pets and farm animals

**Creature: Chicken**  
Page: 5  
The question: Can chickens say more than "cluck"?  
The answer: Yes. They have about 14 different clucking sounds that warn of danger, food and more, and can tell the difference between about 30 different birds, numbers, shapes, patterns and even human faces.

**Creature: Cat**  
Page: 35  
The question: What's odd about a cat's urine?  
The answer: It glows under a blacklight.  
Page: 42  
The question: Why are cats scared of cucumbers?  
The answer: They get startled when anything suddenly appears, especially cucumbers, which the page guesses they mistake for snakes.  
Page: 77  
The question: How many toes does a cat have?  
The answer: 5 on each front foot but only 4 on each back foot.

**Creature: Horse**  
Page: 93  
The question: Can a horse breathe through its mouth?  
The answer: No. Even after a hard race, horses can only breathe through their nostrils.

**Creature: Goat**  
Page: 96  
The question: Can goats read faces?  
The answer: Yes. Researchers showed goats side-by-side pictures of people, one happy and one sad, and the goats gave far more attention to the happy face.

## 🦁 Land: wild mammals

**Creature: Elephant**  
Page: 1  
The question: Do elephants mourn?  
The answer: They show emotions for each other. When an elephant dies, others may try to bury it and come back to that spot long after, as if showing respect or mourning.  
Page: 26  
The question: How strong is an elephant's trunk?  
The answer: It uses its trunk like arms to pick up things, feel things and eat, and the trunk has over 40,000 muscles.

**Creature: Prairie dog**  
Page: 7  
The question: What dog isn't a dog?  
The answer: A prairie dog is a ground squirrel. It lives in underground "cities" (burrows), enjoys cuddles, and "barks" to others in conversations.

**Creature: Bison**  
Page: 8  
The question: When is a buffalo not a buffalo?  
The answer: When it's a bison. Bison have a thick coat, a much larger head with a small beard and big bulging shoulders, and small pointy horns, where buffalo have much longer ones. The American bison was mistakenly called a buffalo.

**Creature: Opossum**  
Page: 25  
The question: Can an animal survive snake bites?  
The answer: It can withstand about 80 bites from a rattlesnake or coral snake without being affected. Studying it has helped make anti-venom.

**Creature: Mouse**  
Page: 41  
The question: Why are male mice scared of bananas?  
The answer: Pregnant or nursing mice give off chemical signals that keep other mice away from their young, and a similar chemical is in bananas.

**Creature: Polar bear**  
Page: 37  
The question: Are polar bears left-handed?  
The answer: The page says right-handed people live about 9 years longer than left-handed ones, and that polar bears are left-handed.

**Creature: Babirusa**  
Page: 46  
The question: What's strange about its tusks?  
The answer: It looks like a wild pig and it is one. Its two tusks begin in the mouth like teeth and then grow right through the skin and come up in the face.

**Creature: Coatimundi**  
Page: 48  
The question: Is a cute coatimundi harmless?  
The answer: It's a kind of raccoon that eats fruit, eggs and small rodents. It's cute but known to be extremely vicious.

**Creature: Bilby**  
Page: 51  
The question: Is it a rabbit or a kangaroo?  
The answer: It looks part rabbit and part kangaroo, with long ears to radiate heat and a long snout, but it's related to the marsupials.

**Creature: Kinkajou**  
Page: 53  
The question: How does it move through the trees?  
The answer: It has a long tongue and a very strong tail. It can climb up it or hang down from it.

**Creature: Sunda colugo**  
Page: 54  
The question: Is a "flying lemur" a lemur that flies?  
The answer: No, it's not a lemur and doesn't fly. It glides with large flaps between its legs, like people in gliding suits.

**Creature: Dik-dik**  
Page: 55  
The question: How does this small antelope mark its territory?  
The answer: It uses sticky tears from its eyes to leave a scent mark, instead of urine.

**Creature: Maned wolf**  
Page: 57  
The question: Is it a wolf or a fox?  
The answer: It's neither. It looks like a super-model in long black boots, with very long legs, and some call it a "fox on stilts." The sentence is cut off on the page after "they are not."

**Creature: Patagonian mara**  
Page: 58  
The question: Is it a rabbit?  
The answer: It's like a large rabbit but more related to a rodent. It has a kangaroo-like face and a dog or rabbit body, with short front legs for digging and long back legs for jumping. Hand-raised ones make wonderful pets and are easy to walk on a leash.

**Creature: Markhor**  
Page: 59  
The question: Is it a goat with screw-shaped antlers?  
The answer: Yes. It's the largest goat species, and the horns can grow 5 feet long.

**Creature: Raccoon dog (tanuki)**  
Page: 74  
The question: Is it a raccoon or a dog?  
The answer: Neither. It's a tanuki, a dog with raccoon colors, that eats frogs, rats, berries and dead animals and raids trash cans. It's a wild animal, not a pet.

**Creature: Koala**  
Page: 83  
The question: Are koalas smart?  
The answer: No. A koala's brain is small and smooth, and if you put its eucalyptus leaf on a napkin or plate, it wouldn't know what to do, since as far as it knows the meal is still on the plant. They don't seem to know what rain is.

**Creature: Lion**  
Page: 87  
The question: How can you tell a lion's age?  
The answer: By its nose. After 3 months it turns pink to light gray, then gets freckles and then liver spots, and at about age 8 it turns black.

**Creature: Marmoset**  
Page: 92  
The question: Do animals use names?  
The answer: Marmosets (a monkey) call each other by name and know when they're being called. Outside of humans this had never been known.

**Creature: Bear**  
Page: 95  
The question: How smart are bears?  
The answer: They solve complex tasks, have a social structure, hide their tracks from hunters, and can remember for years having met another bear only once.

**Creature: Anteater**  
Page: 98  
The question: How do anteaters eat?  
The answer: A long slender nose pokes into an ant village and its tongue stretches up to 24 inches, flicking 150 times a minute, enough to eat 30,000 ants a day. The page begins by comparing aardvarks and anteaters, but only describes the anteater.

## 🌊 Water's edge and semi-aquatic

**Creature: Platypus**  
Page: 30  
The question: What makes the male and female platypus unique?  
The answer: The female is the only mammal that lays eggs (the page says), and the male is the only mammal that produces venom. Stings are rare and are used as a weapon, not for defense.

**Creature: Beaver**  
Page: 31  
The question: Where does strawberry flavor come from?  
The answer: Artificial strawberry flavor comes from the beaver's castor sac (next to its anus), sometimes used to flavor strawberry, raspberry and vanilla scents.

**Creature: Axolotl**  
Page: 49  
The question: Why is this salamander "adorable"?  
The answer: It always looks like it's smiling and keeps its young, juvenile looks all the way through adulthood.

## 🪽 Air and land: birds

**Creature: Birds (general)**  
Page: 20  
The question: How do huge flocks avoid collisions?  
The answer: The page says they never seem to run into each other and gives no answer. The picture shows birds saying "Pardon me" and "Excuse me."  
Page: 22 (Black kites, whistling kites, brown falcons)  
The question: Do any animals use fire?  
The answer: Three birds in Australia make or take fire to the bush so the fire flushes out their prey.  
Page: 23  
The question: Can birds fish with bait?  
The answer: Some birds lay pieces of bread on the water and grab the fish that come to eat it.  
Page: 24 (Flying animals)  
The question: Which non-birds can fly?  
The answer: A flying tree snake, flying fish, paradise tree snake, mobula ray, flying squirrel, crab spider and bats, and the oddest is the flying frog, whose giant feet work like a parachute.

**Creature: Ostrich**  
Page: 36  
The question: How big is an ostrich's brain?  
The answer: It's a really big bird with a really little brain, smaller than its eye.

**Creature: Penguin**  
Page: 39 (Emperor penguin)  
The question: Can a bird live its whole life without touching land?  
The answer: The emperor may be the only one. Males guard the egg for up to 4 months on stored fat, through hurricane-strength storms and temperatures as low as −70°.  
Page: 82  
The question: What does a penguin do when it has to poop?  
The answer: It can't leave the eggs, so it shoots "poop bombs" over 4 feet away. Researchers track this so zookeepers don't get too close.

**Creature: Acorn woodpecker**  
Page: 73  
The question: How does a woodpecker store food?  
The answer: It drills holes in a dead tree, each sized for just one acorn and small enough that other animals can't steal it. One tree might hold 50,000 holes, each with an acorn.

**Creature: Harpy eagle**  
Page: 75  
The question: How strong is it?  
The answer: It's the largest of the eagles, and its talons grip prey like a bear's. It needs large prey to get enough to eat.

**Creature: Shoebill stork**  
Page: 86  
The question: Can you keep a shoebill as a pet?  
The answer: No. It has a very large shoe-shaped bill and isn't aggressive toward people, but it's endangered and shouldn't be bothered.

## 🐞 Insects and bugs

**Creature: Bee**  
Page: 10  
The question: Can a bee take back its sting?  
The answer: The page says a bee may instantly regret stinging you and "unwind" its stinger, pull it out and fly away, leaving you with no swelling.  
Page: 91  
The question: How do bees fight hornets?  
The answer: As many as 500 bees surround a hornet in a ball and vibrate their wing muscles to heat it to 116.6°F for 20 minutes, killing it with their "bee furnace."

**Creature: Dragonfly**  
Page: 19  
The question: How does a female dragonfly turn down a male?  
The answer: She fakes her own death to avoid males she doesn't like.  
Page: 79  
The question: What has 6 legs but can't walk?  
The answer: A dragonfly. Its legs help it take off and land, and nobody knows why it can't walk.

**Creature: Butterfly**  
Page: 32  
The question: Are butterflies as sweet as they look?  
The answer: They eat dung and taste with their feet.

**Creature: Cockroach**  
Page: 33  
The question: Why do we squish a cockroach?  
The answer: A cockroach can live 9 days without its head and then starves to death.

**Creature: Giant devil's flower mantis**  
Page: 76  
The question: How does it catch insects?  
The answer: It's one of the largest praying mantises. It stays very still imitating a flower until an insect comes by, then attacks. It won't harm a person.

**Creature: Grasshopper**  
Page: 80  
The question: Where are a grasshopper's ears?  
The answer: Near where its legs meet the body. Some have as many as 6 ears and can feel sound vibrations from half a mile away.

**Creature: Ant**  
Page: 29 (Zombie fungus)  
The question: Can a fungus control an ant?  
The answer: A fungus enters the ant's body, drains its nutrients and takes over its mind. The ant leaves the nest and secures itself to a plant, and a stalk later grows from its head to produce more fungus.  
Page: 89  
The question: Can ants recognize themselves in a mirror?  
The answer: Yes, the page says tiny ants can, and very few animals can.

**Creature: Termite**  
Page: 99  
The question: What's puzzling about termites?  
The answer: They can eat a wooden house but are soft and die if they dry out. Some are soldiers that can't feed themselves. Those that grow wings are terrible fliers and many die.  
Page: 100  
The question: What are the 3 kinds of termites?  
The answer: Reproductives (king and queen, the only ones with eyes), soldiers (bigger heads, pincer jaws, can squirt sticky glue) and workers (eat wood, carry things, feed everyone).

**Creature: Flea**  
Page: 101  
The question: How long do fleas live?  
The answer: Not long, but they can bite pets and produce millions of babies. The babies need food in a couple of days, but adults can go 2 months without.

## 🕷️ Spiders and other crawlers

**Creature: Tarantula**  
Page: 21 (Blue-legged tarantula)  
The question: Which tarantula is beautiful?  
The answer: One with legs covered in blue hair. It lives with others, and the mothers take good care of their babies.  
Page: 27  
The question: Do tarantulas have pets?  
The answer: In some countries tarantulas have pet frogs. The frog takes shelter under the tarantula, which protects it from predators, and the frog protects the tarantula's eggs from ants.

**Creature: Spider (gift-giving)**  
Page: 38  
The question: Do spiders give gifts?  
The answer: Some male spiders bring a gift, like part of an insect, wrapped in silk, to make a female interested.

**Creature: Peacock spider**  
Page: 47  
The question: How do males attract a mate?  
The answer: They have lots of color (called "sparkle muffins") and dance. They have venom but are harmless to people.

**Creature: Leech**  
Page: 13  
The question: How many brains does a leech have?  
The answer: 32. Its body is divided into 32 segments and each has its own brain. It can go 6 months without eating.  
Page: 14  
The question: Which animals have duplicate parts?  
The answer: Leech with 32 brains, cuttlefish with 3 hearts, cows with a 4-part stomach, Baird's beaked whale with up to 13 stomachs, and the champion, a sea worm (*Ramisyllis multicaudata*) with hundreds of rear ends, each with its own eyes and brain.

**Creature: Worms**  
Page: 78  
The question: Which end of a worm is which?  
The answer: An earthworm's head has a large band below it, and if cut below the band the head can grow a new tail. A planarian flatworm can regrow a whole body, and the new head has all the memories of the first one.

## 🐊 Reptiles and amphibians

**Creature: Burmese peacock softshell turtle**  
Page: 4  
The question: What's strange about this turtle?  
The answer: It has legs like an elephant's trunk, a long pointed snout, little eyes, and "the energy of a sloth," and it can tuck its head into its neck.

**Creature: Turtle (glowing)**  
Page: 84  
The question: Do turtles glow?  
The answer: Some have biofluorescence, which means they reflect light so they look like they glow in the dark.

**Creature: Snake**  
Page: 12  
The question: Can snakes swim?  
The answer: Yes, all snakes can swim. There are over 4,000 species and 70 live in the ocean.

**Creature: Great African land snail**  
Page: 88  
The question: Why is this snail banned in many places?  
The answer: It's huge and harmful to the environment and farmland if it gets loose, so many governments have outlawed owning it.

## 🐋 Sea mammals

**Creature: Whales and dolphins**  
Page: 11  
The question: Which tastes can they sense?  
The answer: People taste sweet, salty, sour, bitter and umami, but whales and dolphins can only taste salt.

## 🐟 Fish and sharks

**Creature: Fish (general)**  
Page: 15  
The question: How do fish schools decide where to go?  
The answer: There are over 36,000 species. Fish swim in large schools with no clear leader, and have to decide who to follow, when and where.  
Page: 16  
The question: Can you tell a fish's age?  
The answer: Fish scales grow differently in summer and winter, making light and dark lines you can count, like tree rings.  
Page: 17  
The question: How do fish communicate?  
The answer: Through chemical signals, electrical systems or light pulses. About 800 species also make sounds under water.  
Page: 18  
The question: Are fish smart?  
The answer: Yes. They can be trained, use tools to get food, and use navigational skills.

**Creature: Ghost shark**  
Page: 3  
The question: Where does it live and what does it look like?  
The answer: On the ocean floor about 1½ miles down. It's also called a "spook fish" or chimaera, with a long snout.

**Creature: Megalodon**  
Page: 6  
The question: How big was a baby megalodon?  
The answer: Over 6½ feet at birth, and it grew to nearly 50 feet, more than twice the size of sharks studied today.

**Creature: Dogfish**  
Page: 9  
The question: Is a dogfish a dog?  
The answer: No, it's a small bottom-dwelling shark that eats small fish, octopus, squid and jellyfish. It has venom from two spines, one in front of each dorsal fin.

**Creature: Catfish**  
Page: 34  
The question: How many taste buds does a catfish have?  
The answer: Over 27,000.

**Creature: Whitemargin stargazer**  
Page: 44  
The question: Why is it rarely seen?  
The answer: It buries itself in the sand with only its eyes sticking out.

**Creature: Ocean sunfish**  
Page: 45  
The question: What does it look like?  
The answer: Many people mistake it for a dolphin's head swimming alone. It's one of the boniest fish in the world.

**Creature: Alligator gar (not named on the page)**  
Page: 52  
The question: Is it a fish or an alligator?  
The answer: It looks like both squished together, with an alligator-like snout, mouth and teeth on a fish body. It doesn't usually harm people unless they try to land or wrestle it.

**Creature: Frogfish**  
Page: 56 (Warty frogfish)  
The question: How does it hide?  
The answer: It looks like a frog with a face covered in warts, and like a chameleon it hides in plain sight against the coral. It's not poisonous like the similar toadfish.  
Page: 62 (Shaggy frogfish)  
The question: How does it hunt?  
The answer: It's a vicious deep-sea predator with a frog-like mouth that tries to swallow things as big as itself, and a lure on its head that others can't resist.

**Creature: Pigfish**  
Page: 61  
The question: Why is it called a pigfish?  
The answer: It grunts when taken from the water, by rubbing its teeth together. It has skin instead of scales, and the skin molts.

**Creature: Hammerhead shark**  
Page: 63  
The question: How does it find prey and give birth?  
The answer: It can grow to 18 feet and finds prey by chemical, electrical and thermal senses. Pups are born in groups of up to 56, all inside the mother.

**Creature: Viperfish**  
Page: 64  
The question: How does its jaw work?  
The answer: It opens 90 degrees and comes crashing down like a steel trap. Its first vertebra acts as a shock absorber.

**Creature: Sixgill shark**  
Page: 66  
The question: Where does it live and hunt?  
The answer: About 1½ miles down by day, rising to the surface at night. It has 6 rows of teeth in its lower jaw for sawing and tearing.

**Creature: Barreleye fish**  
Page: 67  
The question: Can you see inside its head?  
The answer: Yes, its head is transparent, like a snow globe.

**Creature: Hatchetfish**  
Page: 69  
The question: Why is it named for a hatchet?  
The answer: Its head and body look like a small axe head. It can regulate the intensity of its own bioluminescence.

**Creature: Frilled shark**  
Page: 70  
The question: Is it dangerous?  
The answer: Also called the lizard shark, it has a nasty set of teeth but is basically harmless to people, living in deep water.

**Creature: Blind eel**  
Page: 81  
The question: What is the new jelly-bodied eel?  
The answer: Researchers recently found a blind eel with a nearly transparent, jelly-like body and big teeth.

**Creature: Hadal snailfish**  
Page: 85  
The question: How deep does it live?  
The answer: It was thought extinct. It lives 7,000 meters down (nearly 4 miles) and is the 2nd deepest fish ever caught.

**Creature: Clownfish**  
Page: 94  
The question: Are all clownfish born male?  
The answer: Yes. A school is led by a dominant female, and when she dies one of the males turns into a female.

**Creature: Carp**  
Page: 97  
The question: Can eggs survive a bird's stomach?  
The answer: Carp eggs swallowed and eliminated by birds stayed intact and could still hatch.

## 🐙 Sea invertebrates

**Creature: Octopus**  
Page: 2  
The question: Do octopuses act like mobsters?  
The answer: Some work with a fish to attract other fish, and if the chosen partner won't cooperate, the octopus attacks it.

**Creature: Telescope octopus**  
Page: 71  
The question: Is this octopus transparent?  
The answer: Its body is almost transparent, with webbing between the arms, and large tubular eyes that rotate. It tends to stay at one level instead of swimming up, down or sideways.

**Creature: Jellyfish**  
Page: 28  
The question: Can a jellyfish go back to being young?  
The answer: When endangered or injured it can return to the larva stage and start over, so it's almost immortal.  
Page: 68 (Benthocodon)  
The question: How is this jellyfish different?  
The answer: It's small (the dome may be under an inch), well-rounded, red, with about 1,500 red tentacles.

**Creature: Chambered nautilus**  
Page: 60  
The question: Why does it live in a shell?  
The answer: Air-filled compartments help with buoyancy and the animal lives in the last one, reaching out with tentacles for food.

**Creature: Japanese spider crab**  
Page: 65  
The question: How big is the largest arthropod?  
The answer: Up to 12 feet from the end of one arm to the other, about a bedroom's width, and it can live for 100 years.

**Creature: Osedax (zombie worm)**  
Page: 72  
The question: What does a "bone-eater" worm eat?  
The answer: The fats in whale bones. Its favorite food is a whale.

**Creature: Single-celled animal**  
Page: 40  
The question: What lights up New Zealand's beaches?  
The answer: A single-celled animal that glows blue when disturbed, like lightning bugs.

**Creature: Clam**  
Page: 90  
The question: How old can a clam be?  
The answer: Researchers studying a dead clam may have killed one of the longest-living animals. Their best estimate was 405 years old.

## 🔬 Other topics

**Creature: Albino animals**  
Page: 43  
The question: Can animals be albino?  
The answer: Albino means born without pigment. An albino panda was found, and an albino dolphin (mostly pink). There have been white giraffes, whales, gorillas, penguins, koalas and alligators.

## What the book shows overall

- **This book is the most "weird animal parade" of the series.** About 40% of the pages are deep-sea or ocean oddities (pages 56–71 run through frogfish, hammerhead, viperfish, spider crab, sixgill, barreleye, jellyfish, hatchetfish, frilled shark, telescope octopus and osedax).
- **There is a run of "weird fish bodies" and "not what it's named" pages.** Pages 7–9 (prairie dog, bison, dogfish), 46 and 61 (babirusa, pigfish) and 74 (raccoon dog) all tell you the name is misleading.
- **It reads like a sequel to the other books.** Page 71 begins "You recently read about a fish that had a transparent head" (the barreleye on page 67), and page 82 says "We've already learned the penguins are careful guarding their eggs" (page 39).
- **The caption jokes are a second layer.** Examples are the maned wolf's "Whistle like that again and you'll lose your lips," the birds in a flock saying "Pardon me," and the penguin's "IN-COMING!"
- **Two pages stop short on the page itself.** Page 20 asks about flocks but gives no answer. Page 57 breaks off mid-sentence after "they are not."

## Things you may want to check

1. **Repeats from the other books.** Page 13's leech (32 brains) and page 44's stargazer are near word-for-word copies of Crabby pages 47 and 44. Page 6's megalodon follows the "pandas are born small" line from the animal-world book.
2. **Facts that look wrong or unverified.**
   - Page 10 says a bee can "unwind" its stinger and fly away. A honeybee's stinger tears out and the bee dies. Wasps and some other bees can sting repeatedly.
   - Page 30 says only the female platypus lays eggs, but echidnas also lay eggs (and page 50 mentions the echidna is related to the platypus).
   - Page 31 says artificial strawberry flavor comes from the beaver's castor sac. Castoreum is used rarely and is listed as a natural flavor.
   - Page 37's claim that right-handed people live about 9 years longer comes from a flawed 1991 study, and "polar bears are left-handed" is unverified.
   - Page 39 says the emperor penguin "may be the only bird" that never touches land. Emperors do breed on sea ice, and albatrosses spend most of their lives at sea too.
   - Page 41 says cats are scared of cucumbers. This is based on viral videos and a guess.
   - Page 80's "half a mile" grasshopper hearing sounds exaggerated.
   - Page 79's "nobody knows why dragonflies can't walk" overstates it; their legs are built for perching.
   - Page 90's 405-year-old clam is "Ming," a documented case.
3. **Unnamed animals.** Page 52's fish isn't named (it's an alligator gar). Page 20's bird flock and page 84's turtle species aren't named.
4. **Typos I noticed.**
   - "Austrailian" (p12)
   - "Barid's" (p14)
   - "barbirusa" (p46)
   - "anthropod" (p65)
   - "tale" for "tail" (p78)
   - "scarey" (p81)
   - "it's first vertebra" (p64)
   - "Koala's" and "Tanuki's" as plurals (p83, p74)
5. **OCR limits.** The text layer was mostly clean, and I confirmed pages 8, 10, 20, 24, 57 and 62 against the page images.
