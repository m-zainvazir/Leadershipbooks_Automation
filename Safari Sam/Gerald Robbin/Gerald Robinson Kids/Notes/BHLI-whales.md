I read all 101 pages of the *Because He Liked It!* book in the file named "whales in blubber jacket" (the cover title is printed on top of itself, so the OCR can't read it, and despite the file name only two pages are about whales). About 40 pages had scrambled OCR text, so I rendered a sample of them as images and read those directly. Each page follows the usual pattern: a "Why did God do that?" header, a short fact about an animal, and a joke caption from the animal in a speech bubble. The answer is always "Because He liked it!"

The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Creatures that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives.

## 🐾 Land: pets, farm animals and domestic life

**Creature: Goat**  
Page: 2  
The question: Which goat climbs trees?  
The answer: A goat that loves Argan berries, which grow on trees, so it learned to climb the trees to get its favorite food.  
Page: 63  
The question: Can goats read faces?  
The answer: They can tell if someone is happy or sad and spend more time with those who have the happy face.

**Creature: Lamb**  
Page: 31  
The question: What is a "bummer lamb"?  
The answer: If a mother sheep rejects her newborn, she never changes her mind. The lamb acts depressed and hangs its head low, and a rancher or farmer will adopt it and raise it until it can rejoin the sheep.

**Creature: Cow**  
Page: 51  
The question: Do cows speak different languages?  
The answer: Cows from different places differ in the volume, tone and sound of their moos.

**Creature: Dog**  
Page: 59  
The question: Did dogs descend from wolves?  
The answer: The page says many people believe so, but wolves don't have the muscles to make facial expressions like a dog's sad or delighted eyebrow movements.  
Page: 84 (Dalmatian)  
The question: Are dalmatians born spotted?  
The answer: Most are born white. The spots show as they grow, and every dalmatian's spots are unique, like zebra stripes.  
Page: 98  
The question: How good is a dog's nose?  
The answer: Dogs can sniff out drugs, explosives and people, because the smell area in their brain is 40 times bigger than ours.  
Page: 99  
The question: How good is a dog's hearing?  
The answer: They hear a dog whistle people can't, and special ear muscles let them tune in sounds and move their ears in many directions.  
Page: 100  
The question: Can a dog tell when you're sad?  
The answer: Dogs can tell when you're sick, and when you're heart-broken, sad or depressed, and they comfort you by cuddling.  
Page: 101  
The question: Why do dog feet smell like corn chips?  
The answer: Dogs sweat through their paws, and sweat and bacteria clogged in the paws can make them smell like corn, corn chips or popcorn ("Frito feet").

**Creature: Cheetah**  
Page: 82 (Cheetah)  
The question: Do all cheetahs have spots?  
The answer: Some have stripes. A change in their genes made spots become stripes, and some house cats experience the same thing.

## 🦁 Land: wild mammals

**Creature: Armadillo**  
Page: 3  
The question: How does an armadillo protect itself?  
The answer: It has an armor-like shell and curls into a ball with only its armor exposed. When the danger is over, it springs back onto all four legs.  
Page: 22 (Pink fairy armadillo)  
The question: Which armadillo looks like a bunny?  
The answer: It looks like a cuddly bunny without ears, wearing an armor coat. It comes out at night to eat plants, worms and bugs.

**Creature: Sunda colugo**  
Page: 20  
The question: Is a "flying lemur" a lemur that flies?  
The answer: It is not a lemur and can't fly, but it's called "flying" because its arms have a web that unfolds so it can glide about the distance of a football field.

**Creature: Maned wolf**  
Page: 23  
The question: Is it a wolf or a fox?  
The answer: It's neither. It looks a bit like a wolf or fox with large ears, a fox's body and tail, and can stand up to 3 feet tall, much bigger than an average dog.

**Creature: Fennec fox**  
Page: 24  
The question: Why such large ears?  
The answer: For two reasons: to get rid of body heat like an elephant, and to listen for prey that lives underground.

**Creature: Pangolin**  
Page: 32  
The question: Is a pangolin an armadillo?  
The answer: It reminds the author of an armadillo, covered in armor made of keratin, like your fingernails, and it can curl into a ball. It is hunted for its meat, and its scales are used in medicines.

**Creature: Lowland streaked tenrec**  
Page: 37  
The question: What's special about this little animal?  
The answer: Like crickets, it makes sound by rubbing parts together. It looks like a spiny mouse, can have up to 30 babies at a time, and has 36 nipples to feed them.

**Creature: Saiga antelope**  
Page: 38  
The question: What does a saiga look like?  
The answer: A mix of a moose, a camel and an elephant, with two long horns and a most unusual mouth and nose.

**Creature: Snub-nosed monkey**  
Page: 39  
The question: Why is it endangered?  
The answer: It's both odd and adorable, with a snubbed nose, some of them looking like they could drown if it rained. Some have slightly blue faces.

**Creature: Gerenuk**  
Page: 42  
The question: Why is it called a giraffe gazelle?  
The answer: It has a long neck, legs and eyes. It stands on its hind legs like a dog doing a trick to reach higher leaves, like a giraffe.

**Creature: Babirusa**  
Page: 44  
The question: What's odd about its tusks?  
The answer: It's also known as a deer pig. Its tusks extend from the mouth up through the snout and point up over the eyes toward the head.

**Creature: Fossa**  
Page: 48  
The question: What is a fossa?  
The answer: A mix: a cat's head and claws, a mongoose's long body, a monkey's tail, small round ears, and a skunk-like stink gland.

**Creature: Opossum**  
Page: 53  
The question: Why do cars kill more opossums than snakes?  
The answer: A peptide in the opossum neutralizes snake venom, so it suffers no ill effects. The page says honey badgers and squirrels have a similar ability.

**Creature: Pudu**  
Page: 54  
The question: What's the smallest deer?  
The answer: The pudu stands only 12–17 inches tall and weighs as little as 13 lb, and looks more like a long-legged mouse than a deer.

**Creature: Bear**  
Page: 55  
The question: How smart are bears?  
The answer: Big brains: they hide from predators, cover their tracks, solve complex tasks, and may remember creatures they met years earlier.

**Creature: Elephant**  
Page: 57  
The question: Can elephants swim?  
The answer: Yes, they are great swimmers and use their trunk as a snorkel to swim under the water. They can swim 30 miles in a day.

**Creature: Wolf and raven**  
Page: 58  
The question: Are wolves and ravens friends?  
The answer: A raven gets scraps from a wolf's meal, the wolf learns from raven calls where a carcass is, and the birds act as look-outs. They have even been seen playing together.

**Creature: Sloth**  
Page: 60  
The question: Why don't a sloth's hands get tired?  
The answer: A sloth's resting hand is closed, where yours is naturally open, so its hand stays closed around the branch.  
Page: 97  
The question: How many neck bones does a sloth have?  
The answer: Giraffes and many mammals have 7 vertebrae in the neck, but a sloth has as many as 10.

**Creature: Prairie dog**  
Page: 62  
The question: Do prairie dogs describe threats?  
The answer: Their "chee" sound can tell people, coyotes and hawks apart, and even say "here comes a short person wearing a red shirt."

**Creature: Gorilla**  
Page: 64  
The question: How strong is a silverback gorilla?  
The answer: 10–20 times stronger than people, with a bite stronger than a tiger's but not a whale's. The page has a "Bite-O-Meter" chart.

**Creature: African giant pouched rat**  
Page: 69  
The question: Can a rat find bombs?  
The answer: Yes. It's trained to find land mines, is light enough not to set them off, and gets the job done much faster than people using a mine detector.

**Creature: Red kangaroo**  
Page: 76  
The question: How big is a newborn kangaroo?  
The answer: About the size of a jellybean (an inch long), blind, deaf and bald. It spends the next six months in the pouch. Most kangaroos are left-handed.

**Creature: Zebra**  
Page: 78  
The question: What are stripes for?  
The answer: They may act like pest control keeping dangerous flies away. Scientists put a striped suit on a horse, and the flies wouldn't land.

**Creature: Black panther**  
Page: 79  
The question: Is a black panther solid black?  
The answer: A variety of panther and jaguar looks nearly solid black, but close up it has black spots on its black coat.

**Creature: Tiger**  
Page: 80  
The question: Are tigers striped under the fur?  
The answer: Yes, shave a tiger and its skin is striped too.

**Creature: Platypus**  
Page: 85  
The question: How does a platypus hunt underwater?  
The answer: It can't see, hear or smell underwater, but receptor cells in its bill detect motion, pressure changes and electric signals from prey.

## 🪽 Air and land: birds

**Creature: Cassowary**  
Page: 29  
The question: Can a big bird run fast?  
The answer: Like an ostrich or emu it can't fly, but it runs faster than you. A foot with a really long middle toe is like a sharp sword.

**Creature: Kakapo**  
Page: 71  
The question: How loud is a flightless parrot?  
The answer: It works at night and is louder than a howler monkey, about as loud as a jackhammer. It starts with low booms (20–30 of them) and then lets out a high "ching."

**Creature: Peacock**  
Page: 83  
The question: Why do the "eyes" on a peacock's tail hardly move?  
The answer: The male spreads his feathers to find a girlfriend, and when he shakes them the eyes hardly move, as if staring at you.

## 🦇 Air: bats

**Creature: Honduran white bat**  
Page: 28  
The question: Is there a bat with white fur?  
The answer: Yes, about 2 inches long. It cuts leaves with its teeth to make a tent and eats only one species of fig.

**Creature: Loud bat (the greater bulldog bat)**  
Page: 72  
The question: How loud is a small bat?  
The answer: Louder than the howler monkey and the kakapo, as loud as fireworks or a jet plane getting ready to take off. The next page names it the greater bulldog bat.

## 🐞 Insects and bugs

**Creature: Termite**  
Page: 6  
The question: What else do termites eat?  
The answer: They eat paper money. Termites once broke into a bank and ate a lot of the money. Like ants, they have jobs: workers and soldiers.  
Page: 7  
The question: How many termites are there?  
The answer: More than people, and together they'd weigh over 10 times more than all the people.  
Page: 8  
The question: How tall are termite mounds?  
The answer: Mounds can be 9 feet or more, and some in Africa reach 30 feet, like us building a giant skyscraper.

**Creature: Hummingbird hawk-moth**  
Page: 26  
The question: How is it like a hummingbird?  
The answer: Its wings beat about 70 times a second and hum or buzz. It has a tongue about twice the length of its body to reach inside flowers.

**Creature: Panda ant**  
Page: 34  
The question: Is it a panda or an ant?  
The answer: Neither. It's a very unusual wasp without wings.

**Creature: Ant**  
Page: 52  
The question: How do ants communicate?  
The answer: By vomiting into each other's mouths. Ants have two stomachs, one for their own food and one for another ant's.  
Page: 61 (Exploding ant)  
The question: Which ant sacrifices itself?  
The answer: It tackles an invader and squeezes its abdomen muscles until it explodes, splashing yellow goo on the invader, which is branded for life with the goo and an exploded ant.

**Creature: Luna moth**  
Page: 70  
The question: How does its tail protect it?  
The answer: Its long, beautiful wings have long tails that flutter and confuse a bat's sonar, so the bat bites the tail instead of the body, and the moth escapes with a bit missing.

**Creature: Ladybug**  
Page: 81  
The question: Are all ladybugs red with black dots?  
The answer: No. They come in other colors and patterns, with yellow or black wings, a vertical black band, a wavy pattern, and even a 3-banded lady beetle with 3 black stripes on each wing.

**Creature: Bee**  
Page: 89  
The question: How do bees organize?  
The answer: A hive may have 20,000–80,000 bees with a "chore chart." The queen lays thousands of eggs a day, some hunt for food, and some are undertakers who remove dead bees.  
Page: 90  
The question: Which bees sting?  
The answer: Only the females can sting, and they also do most of the work.  
Page: 91  
The question: How do bees choose a new home?  
The answer: Some bees suggest a place with a "waggle" dance, and the swarm moves only when the entire hive agrees.  
Page: 92  
The question: How high can bees fly?  
The answer: Higher than Mount Everest.

## 🕷️ Spiders and other crawlers

**Creature: Brazilian wandering spider**  
Page: 15  
The question: Why is it so dangerous?  
The answer: Also called the banana spider, the Guinness Book of Records lists it among the most venomous spiders. A very small amount of venom kills a mouse. It hides by day and wanders at night.

**Creature: Death stalker scorpion**  
Page: 13  
The question: How dangerous is it?  
The answer: Its sting might not kill a healthy adult, but it causes a lot of pain and could kill an elderly or small person. The venom has been found helpful for treating brain tumors and diabetes.

**Creature: Spiny orb weaver**  
Page: 45  
The question: How does it hunt?  
The answer: It looks like a tick with thorns around the edges. Each day it eats its old web and builds a new one, and some orb weavers produce sticky droplets with a smell that attracts moths.

**Creature: Cartwheeling spider**  
Page: 75  
The question: How does it escape a wasp?  
The answer: It works at night and doesn't make a web, only a hideaway. While building it could be stung and killed by a wasp, but it escapes by doing cartwheels down a sandy slope.

## 🐊 Reptiles and amphibians

**Creature: Frog**  
Page: 17 (Poison dart frog)  
The question: Can a little frog be dangerous?  
The answer: It's called the most poisonous animal on earth. It's only 2 inches long but can kill 10 adults with a bit of poison that fits on a pinhead. The poison is in its skin, so it's dangerous to touch.  
Page: 18 (Poisoned frogs)  
The question: What does a poisoned frog do?  
The answer: It can't vomit, so it throws out its whole stomach, wipes off the poison with its front feet, and swallows its stomach again.

**Creature: Indian purple frog**  
Page: 25  
The question: How does it raise its young?  
The answer: It's also called a pig-nosed frog. The mother lays as many as 3,000 eggs, and in a day or two they hatch and use their large mouths to suck onto cliffs behind waterfalls.

**Creature: Glass frog**  
Page: 47  
The question: Why "glass" frog?  
The answer: Its body is transparent, like glass you can see through. The page adds that you can see inside it if you look at it from underneath (the OCR for that line is scrambled).

**Creature: Rock toad (rock frog)**  
Page: 49  
The question: How does it hide?  
The answer: Its flat body lets it hide under rocks. Researchers once thought it had no lungs and absorbed oxygen through its skin, but it has very tiny lungs.

**Creature: Cobra**  
Page: 10  
The question: Is a cobra's venom the strongest?  
The answer: A cobra can inflate its neck like a hood. Its venom can easily kill a person or even an elephant. Other snakes' venom may be stronger, but the cobra can inject more.

**Creature: Inland taipan**  
Page: 16  
The question: How dangerous is it?  
The answer: A single bite has enough venom to kill 100 adults, venom 200–400 times a cobra's. No record exists of it killing people.

**Creature: Spiny bush viper**  
Page: 30  
The question: Why can't it back up?  
The answer: It's venomous and has lots of spiny scales, so when it tries to back up, the scales get caught on things.

**Creature: Snake**  
Page: 95  
The question: Do snakes sleep with their eyes open?  
The answer: Snakes have no eyelids, so they can't close their eyes.

**Creature: Satanic leaf-tailed gecko**  
Page: 41  
The question: Why that name?  
The answer: It looks like a leaf, and its long spines earned the name "satan." It can flatten its body to lessen its shadow and open its mouth wide to show a bright red mouth.

**Creature: Giant softshell turtle**  
Page: 40  
The question: How is it different from other turtles?  
The answer: Its shell is soft and leathery. It stays buried until it needs to breathe, and can be 3 feet long and weigh over 200 pounds.

**Creature: Espanola giant tortoise**  
Page: 56  
The question: How close was it to extinction?  
The answer: Other animals ate or destroyed its food and shelter, and at one time one tortoise might not see another for decades. Now there are an estimated 2,000.

**Creature: River turtle**  
Page: 96  
The question: Can turtles talk?  
The answer: They have no vocal cords but make hoots, clicks and clucks you can hear with an underwater microphone, even before they hatch.

## 🐋 Sea mammals

**Creature: Sperm whale**  
Page: 1  
The question: How does the loudest animal make its sound?  
The answer: It inhales through its right nasal cavity to a pair of "clappers" called phonic lips or monkey lips. When they suddenly shut, they make a very loud "click." In theory it's loud enough to kill humans.  
Page: 5  
The question: How can a deep-diving whale survive the pressure?  
The answer: It eats squid with lots of teeth, and the pressure of the deep would pop its eyes, so it can retract them into its body. Swimmers nearby must be careful because its sound can burst their eardrums.

**Creature: Irrawaddy dolphin**  
Page: 46  
The question: Which dolphin has no beak?  
The answer: Its head looks a little misshapen, and movable lips let it make expressions like a smile or a frown.

**Creature: Atlantic spotted dolphin**  
Page: 73  
The question: Which dolphin is loudest?  
The answer: It's louder than all other dolphins, thanks to special fatty tissue in its head that scatters the sound waves, and louder than the howler monkey, the kakapo and the greater bulldog bat.

## 🐟 Fish and sharks

**Creature: Stonefish**  
Page: 14  
The question: How bad is its sting?  
The answer: It's ugly and gruesome, and has been called the worst pain known to mankind, so bad a person might want an arm or leg taken off.

**Creature: Goblin shark**  
Page: 21  
The question: What does a goblin shark look like?  
The answer: A long jaw and nail-like teeth to eat prey. The page adds a speed and distance fact ("about 10 feet," "just 1 second"), but the OCR is scrambled there and I couldn't read the exact wording.

**Creature: Red-lipped batfish**  
Page: 27  
The question: Does it swim?  
The answer: It walks more than it swims, using its pectoral fins like tiny feet, and has very red lips.

**Creature: Dwarf lantern shark**  
Page: 77  
The question: How small is it?  
The answer: Under 8 inches fully grown. Like the lightning bug, chemicals in its skin make light, so it's called a "lantern" shark.

**Creature: Garnai fish (flying fish)**  
Page: 88  
The question: Which fish can fly, swim and walk?  
The answer: It can launch itself out of the water at 35 mph and glide as far as 650 ft, longer than 2 football fields.

**Creature: Rock greenling**  
Page: 94  
The question: Why is a blue fish called a greenling?  
The answer: The color comes from a green bile, though scientists aren't sure why the fish produce it.

**Creature: Carp**  
Page: 65  
The question: How do carp get into ponds?  
The answer: Carp eggs survive a bird's digestion and hatch wherever the bird poops them out. The mother carp lays up to 1.5 million eggs, several times a season.

**Creature: Marbled cone snail**

**Creature: Blue-ringed octopus**

**Creature: Blue dragon (sea slug, not named)**

**Creature: Dumbo octopus**

**Creature: Blanket octopus**  
Page: 87

The answer: A web connects 4 of its 8 arms and spreads like a parachute to make it look bigger. It can also rip off the tentacles of a man-of-war and use them like a whip.

**Creature: Vampire squid**  
Page: 36  
The question: Is it a squid or a vampire?

**Creature: Yeti crab**  
Page: 50  
The question: Why "yeti"?

**Creature: Sea pig**  
Page: 43  
The question: What is a sea pig?  
The answer: More like a sea cucumber, living on the ocean floor about a half-mile down. It inflates its legs with seawater to walk on the bottom.

**Creature: Fairy shrimp**  
Page: 93  
The question: How does it survive drought?  
The answer: It can live as an egg for years during a drought.

**Creature: Snail**  
Page: 33 (Kaputar pink slug)  
The question: Where can you find a pink slug?  
The answer: Only on Mount Kaputar in Australia. It's an unusual shade of pink and comes out at night in the hundreds to eat tree moss and algae.  
Page: 74 (*Rumina decollata*)  
The question: How long can a snail go without eating?  
The answer: A snail glued to a card at the British Museum in the 1800s was found alive after 4 years.  
Page: 86  
The question: Why is snail slime in beauty products?  
The answer: The trail is made of 3 types of mucus, which is used in many cosmetics.

**Creature: Ice worm**  
Page: 4  
The question: What lives on a glacier?  
The answer: An arctic worm, as many as 1,000 per square yard of ice. They eat snow algae and don't freeze because of an anti-freeze protein.

## What the book shows overall

- **The first fifth is a "venom and danger" run.** Pages 9–19 cover box jellyfish, cobra, marbled cone snail, blue-ringed octopus, death stalker, stonefish, Brazilian wandering spider, inland taipan, poison dart frog and sea slug, one after another.
- **The next run is "odd faces and names".** Pages 20–50 are mostly animals that look like something else (colugo, pink fairy armadillo, maned wolf, pangolin, saiga, snub-nosed monkey, gerenuk, babirusa, fossa, yeti crab).
- **There are several themed runs.** Pages 6–8 (termites), 66–68 (octopus), 71–73 (loud animals: kakapo, bat, dolphin), 78–82 (stripes and spots), 89–92 (bees) and 98–101 (dogs).
- **The "whales" in the title are mostly absent.** Only pages 1 and 5 are about the sperm whale, and page 64's gorilla chart mentions a whale's bite.
- **The caption jokes are a second layer.** Examples are the cobra's "go big or go home," the pink fairy armadillo as the "Wart" Fairy, and the flying fish "marathon" runners with numbers on them.

## Things you may want to check

1. **Repeats from the other books.** Page 20's colugo repeats Elephants page 54, page 23's maned wolf repeats Elephants page 57, page 44's babirusa repeats Elephants page 46, page 55's bears repeat Elephants page 95, page 63's goats repeat Elephants page 96, and page 65's carp eggs repeat Elephants page 97. Page 57's swimming elephants repeat page 29 of the animal-world book, and page 24's fennec fox repeats page 99 of the Crocs book.
2. **Facts that look wrong or unverified.**
   - Page 5 says sperm whales retract their eyes to avoid popping. No source supports this.
   - Page 7 says termites would weigh over 10 times more than all people. Estimates are closer to the same order of magnitude.
   - Page 59 says wolves can't make dog-like facial expressions, which is partly true (dogs have an eyebrow muscle wolves lack), but dogs did descend from wolves.
   - Page 37's 36 nipples for the lowland streaked tenrec looks too high. Tenrecs have about 24–29.
   - Page 97's "as many as 10 neck vertebrae" for a sloth is usually given as 9 for three-toed sloths.
   - Page 71's "as loud as a jackhammer" for the kakapo is a loose comparison.
   - Page 57's elephants swimming 30 miles a day is unverified.
   - Page 53's "cars kill more opossums than snakes" is a loose claim.
   - Page 14's stonefish quote ("worst pain known") is a common claim, not an established measurement.
3. **Unnamed animals.** Page 19's sea slug isn't named (the picture shows a blue dragon, *Glaucus*). Page 72's bat isn't named on its own page. Page 88's "garnai" fish isn't a standard name, and the picture shows a flying fish.
4. **Typos I noticed.**
   - "decejving" (p9)
   - "embarassing" (p27)
   - "artic" (p4)
   - "definitly" (p39)
   - "banna" (p15)
   - "stareing" (p95)
   - "Sundo Colugo" for "Sunda" (p20)
   - "cartwheelng" (p75)
   - "banket" (p87)
   - "weigh" for "weight" (p69)
5. **OCR limits.** About 40 pages had scrambled text. I confirmed pages 10, 19, 20, 34, 49, 66, 69, 75 and 88 against the page images and read the rest from the OCR, so a few details (for example page 21's goblin shark numbers) may be slightly off.
