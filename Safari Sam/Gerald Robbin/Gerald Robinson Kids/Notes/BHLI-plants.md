I read all 101 pages of *Because He Liked It: Plants* (the cover title is printed on top of itself, so the OCR can't read it, but the book is about plants). The text layer was mostly clean, and I checked a few pages against the page images. This book is laid out differently from the animal books: each page has a "Why did God do that?" header, a bold title naming the plant, and a short fact or caption over a painted picture, with a small Safari Sam in the corner. There are no speech-bubble jokes. The answer is always "Because He liked it!"

The "creatures" in this file are plants, so each heading is a plant or topic. About 20 pages are picture-only pages with just a plant name and no text. The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Plants that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives. For picture-only pages, the answer is the caption alone.

## 🪤 Meat-eating plants

**Creature: Butterwort**  
Page: 1  
The question: How does it catch bugs?  
The answer: It has a beautiful flower (usually purple) that makes a sticky mucus that attracts bugs. They get stuck and are digested by the plant.

**Creature: Sundew**  
Page: 17  
The question: How does a plant "eat meat"?  
The answer: Its colorful tentacles are covered in sticky mucus. When an insect touches one, it's trapped and the plant rolls up around it, so more of the insect touches the plant, which sucks out the nutrients.

**Creature: Venus fly-trap**  
Page: 18  
The question: How does the best-known meat-eating plant work?  
The answer: When a bug lands on the leaves or the "hairs," the arms don't close right away. They wait for a second touch and then snap shut and hold the bug until it's digested.

**Creature: Pitcher plants (monkey cup)**  
Page: 19 (Monkey cup)  
The question: How does a pitcher trap work?  
The answer: It makes a pitcher with a lid, filled with liquid that attracts mites, mosquitoes and flies, and even a frog. It can't digest a frog, but it takes nutrients from the frog's poop.  
Page: 23 (White pitcher plant)  
The question: Why are there few white pitcher plants left?  
The answer: It's beautiful, so too many were collected. Like other carnivorous plants, it collects rainwater, adds enzymes, and the insect drowns and is digested.  
Page: 24 (Sun pitcher plant)  
The question: How does it attract insects?  
The answer: Beautiful colors make it sought after. It grows in bunches, red tips give off nectar and aroma, and bugs stuck in the sticky goo are digested by enzymes.  
Page: 25 (Albany pitcher plant)  
The question: Which plant looks like the movie "Little Shop of Horrors"?  
The answer: The Albany pitcher plant. It doesn't use rainwater. Insects get stuck on contact, and it also has "teeth" and a lid that make entering easy and leaving nearly impossible.  
Page: 28 (More pitcher plants)  
The question: What colors are pitcher plants?  
The answer: Yellow, purple and tropical, in different shapes. The tropical one resembles a toilet.

**Creature: Bladderwort**  
Page: 20  
The question: How does it trap bugs?  
The answer: It has bladders powered by a vacuum. It gives off mucus and sugar to attract lunch, and when a tiny bug triggers a bladder it springs shut and the vacuum draws in the water and the bug.

**Creature: Cobra lily**  
Page: 21  
The question: Is a cobra lily a lily?  
The answer: No. The "cobra" comes from the hood-shaped flower. Its trap water comes up from its roots, not rain, and an enzyme digests the food.

**Creature: Rainbow plant**  
Page: 26  
The question: How does a plant with no pitcher catch bugs?  
The answer: It has very sticky goo that shows rainbow colors in the sun. It catches bugs too big to eat and uses them as bait for smaller bugs, whose droppings are what the plant eats.

**Creature: Waterwheel**  
Page: 27  
The question: How is it like a Venus fly-trap?  
The answer: Its leaves snap shut when they sense movement, but it lives in water and collects aquatic critters. It's one of the fastest-moving plants.

**Creature: White strap airplant**  
Page: 29  
The question: How does it catch bugs?  
The answer: It has several bowls that collect rainwater, and a white powder on its leaves makes them slippery so bugs fall in.

**Creature: Jack-in-the-pulpit**  
Page: 85  
The question: How does it use gnats?  
The answer: It imitates a mushroom (the gnats' favorite snack). The gnat enters, gets stuck on the waxy pitcher and dies, but lays eggs first, and the eggs feed on the flower until new gnats fly away.

## 🗣️ Plants that "talk," warn and defend

**Creature: Grass**  
Page: 4  
The question: Why does cut grass smell?  
The answer: The smell is a distress signal from the plants. Many plants give off a smell when in danger or hurt, like being cut by a lawnmower.

**Creature: Cucumber**  
Page: 30  
The question: Do plants make noise when hurt?  
The answer: The page says cucumbers in distress make a noise as they release gas, as a warning. A laser-powered microphone picks it up, so cucumbers "scream" and flowers "whine."

**Creature: Tomato**  
Page: 15  
The question: How many genes does a tomato have?  
The answer: People have about 20,000–25,000 genes, and tomatoes have far more.  
Page: 31  
The question: Do tomatoes make sounds?  
The answer: Stressed tomatoes can give off ultrasonic sounds when dehydrated or damaged, heard with special microphones up to 4 inches away. It's like popping bubble wrap or tap dancing.

**Creature: Plants being chewed**  
Page: 32  
The question: Do plants feel pain?  
The answer: No, because they have no brain or nervous system. But when a caterpillar chews, a plant can sense it and lets out a little gas that makes a noise.

**Creature: Plant whisperers**  
Page: 83  
The question: Do plants warn each other?  
The answer: Scientists in Japan caught plants sending out warning scents when bugs attack. They tweaked a plant to glow when stressed, and it lit up "like a Christmas tree."

**Creature: Acacia tree**  
Page: 51  
The question: How do acacias defend themselves?  
The answer: In South Africa 3,000 antelope were found dead. When leaves are eaten, the tree releases ethylene that travels 50 yards, and within 10 minutes other acacias boost the tannin in their leaves and make them poisonous.  
Page: 89  
The question: Same page again.  
The answer: A word-for-word repeat of page 51.  
Page: 94 (Apple-ring acacia)  
The question: Which tree does things backwards?  
The answer: It drops its leaves at the start of the growing season, not the end, so the decaying leaves fertilize the ground. Maize grown under it increased the harvest by nearly 300 percent.

## 🍎 Fruits, vegetables, nuts and spices

**Creature: Apple**  
Page: 2  
The question: Do apple seeds grow the same apple?  
The answer: No. They may grow a different kind, because of different chromosomes. The page says God made them that way so different seeds give better chances of survival in poor soil.

**Creature: Lemon**  
Page: 3  
The question: Did God create lemons?  
The answer: No. Farmers crossed sour oranges and citrons to make lemons.

**Creature: Green bell pepper**  
Page: 5  
The question: Are green and red peppers different?  
The answer: A green pepper is just a red one that hasn't ripened. Taste changes too: orange, yellow and green peppers get sweeter and carry more vitamins as they ripen.

**Creature: Pistachio**  
Page: 6  
The question: Can nuts burst into flames?  
The answer: Pistachios have a lot of fat. When dry and under pressure, they can heat each other and combust, but they won't "blow up" on you.

**Creature: Pineapple**  
Page: 7  
The question: Why does your tongue tingle?  
The answer: The pineapple "eats back," breaking down protein in your mouth. It was once reserved for royalty and later used as a gift.

**Creature: Carrot**  
Page: 8  
The question: What color were carrots?  
The answer: Originally purple, then yellow, then orange. Today there are more than 40 varieties.

**Creature: Banana**  
Page: 9  
The question: Are bananas radioactive?  
The answer: Yes, a tiny bit. A truck full has set off radiation detectors. You'd have to eat a million at once to be harmed, and your body is already 280 times more radioactive than a banana.  
Page: 36  
The question: Do bananas glow?  
The answer: They glow blue under an ultraviolet light.  
Page: 70  
The question: Is a banana a fruit?  
The answer: A banana is really a berry, so a fruit salad with banana is a berry salad.

**Creature: Nutmeg**  
Page: 10  
The question: What does nutmeg do for you?  
The answer: The page says it can relieve pain, detoxify the body, help skin and ease tooth and gum pains. It comes from the nut of a tropical evergreen tree.

**Creature: Honey**  
Page: 11  
The question: Why does honey never go bad?  
The answer: Bees make it from plants, and it never goes bad. Honey found in tombs thousands of years old could still be eaten.

**Creature: Plant milk**  
Page: 12  
The question: Is milk always from cows?  
The answer: No. Soy, almond and coconut milk come from plants, and the word "lettuce" comes from a word meaning "milk."

**Creature: Nuts**

The answer: Nuts are fruit with a hard shell and a single seed: cashews, peanuts, pistachios, hazelnuts and chestnuts.

**Creature: Eggplant**

**Creature: Saffron**

**Creature: Watermelon**

**Creature: Mango**

**Creature: Peach and almond**

**Creature: Sweet potato and yam**

**Creature: Corn**  
Page: 80  
The question: Can you hear corn grow?

**Creature: Garlic**

The question: Can you taste garlic through your feet?  
The answer: Yes. A chemical called allicin is absorbed through the skin, carried by the bloodstream to your mouth, and tasted there.

**Creature: Walnut**

**Creature: Pecan**

**Creature: Maple tree**

**Creature: Oak tree**

**Creature: Coconut palm**

## 🌳 Trees, seeds and records

**Creature: Oldest living tree**  
Page: 22  
The question: Which is the oldest tree?  
The answer: Methuselah is thought to be, but a nearby Great Basin pine is figured to be 5,062 years old, and Methuselah is 4,845. Its location is a closely guarded secret.

**Creature: Seeds**  
Page: 39 (2,000-year-old seed)  
The question: Can an old seed still grow?  
The answer: Date-palm seeds found at Masada in Israel sprouted after 2,000 dry years. The new tree was named Methuselah after the oldest man in the Bible.  
Page: 40 (32,000-year-old seed)  
The question: Are there older seeds?  
The answer: Russian scientists found seeds they think are several thousand years old, and might revive other plants from long ago.  
Page: 41 (World's largest seed)  
The question: How big is the biggest seed?  
The answer: The coco de mer palm seed is over a foot long and can weigh 55 pounds (3 bowling balls). The tree takes 50 years to reproduce and 10 years for the fruit to ripen.

**Creature: Forest fires**  
Page: 33  
The question: Can fire help plants?  
The answer: Yes. Fire clears the weeds and competing plants, and ash adds nutrients, so healthy young trees often grow faster afterward.

**Creature: Tallest tree**  
Page: 34  
The question: Which is the world's tallest tree?  
The answer: "Hyperion," over 380 feet, about a 38-story building. There may be 3 trillion trees on Earth, more than stars in our galaxy.  
Page: 64 (Redwoods)  
The question: How do tall redwoods stand up?  
The answer: They are the tallest living things (about a 38-story building) but their roots are only 6–12 feet deep, spreading out 100 feet.

**Creature: Ginkgo biloba**  
Page: 35  
The question: How tough is a ginkgo?  
The answer: When the atomic bomb hit Hiroshima, ginkgo trees bloomed the next year.

**Creature: Diptera-oleifera**  
Page: 58  
The question: Can a tree survive lightning?  
The answer: A Panama tree seems to grow stronger with each lightning zap, because the lightning kills parasite vines and competing trees.

**Creature: Dandelion**  
Page: 59  
The question: What else can dandelions do?  
The answer: They might save the rubber supply from a fungus attacking rubber trees. Researchers once tried making tires from them, but it didn't work well.

**Creature: Autumn leaves**  
Page: 63  
The question: Why do leaves change color?  
The answer: They're showing colors that were always there. In spring the chlorophyll works so hard that green outshines the other pigments.

**Creature: Sunflower**  
Page: 68  
The question: How does a sunflower follow the sun?  
The answer: As the sun moves west, the east side grows faster, which pushes the head west. In a sped-up film it would look like it was wiggling.

**Creature: Pando**  
Page: 69  
The question: Which forest is one tree?  
The answer: An aspen forest from a single seed and root system, covering over 100 acres with about 47,000 trees that are genetically identical.

**Creature: Baobab**  
Page: 96  
The question: Why is it called the "Tree of Life"?  
The answer: It can be 3,000 years old with a very big trunk and small branches at the top, and it provides food, water and shelter.

**Creature: Bent trees**  
Page: 90  
The question: Why are these trees bent?  
The answer: Nobody knows. A forest of them was found and it's unclear whether it was a storm, a disease or normal for them. A mystery.

**Creature: Death Valley wildflowers**  
Page: 95  
The question: How do desert seeds wait?  
The answer: A waxy coating protects them until rain washes it away, and then millions of blooms cover the area.

**Creature: Mad honey (rhododendron)**

## What the book shows overall

- **It's the first book in the series with a theme.** Pages 1, 17–29 and 85 run through meat-eating plants, pages 43–56 and 60–62 through flowers that look like things, pages 30–32 and 83 through plants that "talk," and pages 38 and 65–67 through poisons.
- **Picture pages with almost no text.** Pages 52–56 and 60–62 only show a plant and its name, so a reader has to supply the "why."
- **It repeats itself.** Pages 51 and 89 are the same Acacia page, and bananas appear three times (9, 36, 70).
- **It refers back.** Page 30 says "Previously, you may have learned that fresh cut grass makes a smell" (page 4), and page 83 says "We've already learned that cut grass sends out a warning."
- **There is no joke layer here.** Safari Sam appears only as a small figure, and there are no speech bubbles.

## Things you may want to check

1. **Repeats.** Pages 51 and 89 are word for word the same. Page 13's chicken and T. rex fact is also in the Crabby book (page 54), and page 70's banana-as-berry repeats page 9's banana theme.
2. **Facts that look wrong or unverified.**
   - Page 85 calls Jack-in-the-pulpit a "meat-eating plant." It traps gnats for pollination, and it isn't carnivorous.
   - Page 72 calls Welwitschia "one of the biggest flowers in the world." It is a gymnosperm with cones, not a flowering plant.
   - Page 30's "cucumbers scream and flowers whine" isn't established. The 2023 study showed tomato and tobacco plants clicking.
   - Page 5 says green, orange and yellow peppers are all unripe red ones. Peppers come in several true color varieties.
   - Page 10's health claims for nutmeg are unproven, and large doses can be toxic.
   - Page 40's title says 32,000 years but the text says "several thousand." The famous 32,000-year-old Russian seed was *Silene stenophylla*.
   - Page 79's claim that falling coconuts kill 150 people a year is a widely repeated myth.
   - Page 86's "over 200 years" for a Lazarus plant and page 87's bubble plant are unverified.
   - Page 99's "karyon" meaning "head" is doubtful. The Greek word *karyon* means "nut."
   - Page 38's "Poison Park" is the Alnwick Poison Garden in England.
   - Page 22 says the Great Basin pine nearby is 5,062 years old. The page doesn't name it, and the location is secret.
3. **Unnamed species.** Pages 52–56 and 60–62 give only a common name and no species, so the "Darth Vader" flower and "Rare Naked Man" can't be identified from the page. The painting on page 61 looks like a moth orchid, not a naked-man orchid.
4. **Typos I noticed.**
   - "differerence" (p27)
   - "Lazaarus" (p86)
   - "radio active" (p9)
   - "sunt" (p26)
   - "Egyption" does not appear here
   - "Hydnora africanaplant" (p76 title)
   - "the Rainbow plant the Rainbow plant" (p26)
   - "it's location" (p22)
   - "an other-worldly" (p96)
5. **OCR limits.** The text was mostly clean. I checked pages 1, 13, 54, 60 and 61 against the page images. I haven't seen pictures on most pages, so the captions for page 55 ("Skeleton Grass Snapdragon") and page 56 ("Dove") might be one or two flowers.
