I read all 101 pages of the *Because He Liked It!* book in the file named "worms interior" (the cover title is printed on top of itself, so the OCR can't read it, and despite the file name only pages 1 and 100 are about worms). The text layer was clean for nearly every page, so I used it directly and did not need to render pages as images. Each page follows the usual pattern: a "Why did God do that?" header, a short fact about an animal, and a joke caption from the animal in a speech bubble. The answer is always "Because He liked it!"

The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Creatures that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives.

## 🐾 Land: pets and farm animals

**Creature: Cow**  
Page: 5  
The question: How well can a cow see?  
The answer: Its eyes are on the sides of its face, so it can see almost all the way around, except directly behind. It has poor depth perception, so it's nearly impossible to get a cow to walk down stairs.

**Creature: Cat**  
Page: 22  
The question: Why don't cats "talk" to each other?  
The answer: Dogs bark at each other, people and anything that moves, but cats only meow to get a human's attention and don't meow at each other.  
Page: 30  
The question: Can cats taste sugar?  
The answer: No. A recent study found cats don't have a sweet tooth.  
Page: 42  
The question: Can a cat be allergic to people?  
The answer: Some cats show signs of allergies near human dandruff.

**Creature: Horse**  
Page: 41  
The question: Who are a horse's cousins?  
The answer: Horses are distantly related to rhinoceroses and tapirs, though none of the three look alike.

## 🦁 Land: wild mammals

**Creature: Hippo**  
Page: 2  
The question: How sociable are hippos?  
The answer: They live in pods of one male, many females and babies, and fight any other male who tries to join. They defend the pod, lake or river and can chase and crush people. At night they spread out to graze alone.  
Page: 68 (Pygmy hippo)  
The question: How long does a pygmy hippo eat?  
The answer: 6 hours a day, with 4 stomachs like cows. It weighs 350–600 pounds but can run up to 18 mph.

**Creature: Elephant**  
Page: 19  
The question: Do baby elephants suck their trunks?  
The answer: Yes, like a baby sucking a thumb, for comfort when stressed.

**Creature: Bear**  
Page: 20  
The question: Should you play dead if a bear finds you?  
The answer: The page says the latest advice is to run, because a bear's bite can crush a bowling ball.

**Creature: Prairie dog**  
Page: 21  
The question: How do prairie dogs greet someone new?  
The answer: They "kiss," though the page jokes you shouldn't try it or you could be arrested.

**Creature: Capuchin monkey**  
Page: 18  
The question: How does a monkey wash its feet?  
The answer: It pees on its hand to wash its feet.

**Creature: Fox**  
Page: 31  
The question: Is a fox like other dogs?  
The answer: No. It's most active at night, pounces like a cat, uses whiskers like a cat, has spines on its tongue, and some have retractable claws to climb trees and sleep in branches.  
Page: 32 (Arctic fox)  
The question: How cold can an arctic fox stand?  
The answer: Its fur is so thick it doesn't shiver until −94°F (−70°C), and then it wraps its tail around itself like a blanket.

**Creature: Chimpanzee**  
Page: 48  
The question: Do chimps act as doctors?  
The answer: Some chimps self-medicate with plants, and now they've been seen giving first aid to others as nurses and doctors. They also show good hygiene, like wiping themselves.

**Creature: Opossum**  
Page: 51  
The question: How does a newborn opossum feed?  
The answer: It's about the size of a honeybee and doesn't know how to suck, so the mother tightens her muscles to push, squirt or pump milk into the baby.  
Page: 52  
The question: Are opossums clean?  
The answer: Yes. They bathe often and clean up when eating, even though they eat rotting flesh.

**Creature: Anteater**  
Page: 27  
The question: How does an anteater eat without teeth?  
The answer: Its long sticky tongue gathers the ants, and its stomach muscles crush them once swallowed.

**Creature: Marsupial mole**  
Page: 66  
The question: Why does its pouch face backward?  
The answer: A forward-facing pouch would scoop up dirt as it digs, so God made the pouch face backward.

**Creature: Cape buffalo**  
Page: 67  
The question: How big are its horns?  
The answer: Up to 58 inches across, nearly 6 feet. It eats plants but may chase people it thinks are predators.  
Page: 84 (Vote)  
The question: Do buffalo vote?  
The answer: Yes, only the adult females. When it's time to travel, they stand, look in one direction and lie back down, which "votes" on the way to go.

**Creature: Leopard**  
Page: 69  
The question: How big is a leopard?  
The answer: About 2 feet tall at the shoulder but up to 7 feet long, and up to 125 pounds. It stays away from you unless made anxious or afraid.

**Creature: Rat**  
Page: 8 (Giant African rat)  
The question: Can a rat sniff out smuggled goods?  
The answer: These rats grow to almost 3 feet long, and are trained to sniff out rhino horns, pangolin scales and elephant tusks in warehouses and shipyards.  
Page: 71  
The question: Why were rats a plague?  
The answer: A pair can produce as many as 2,000 babies in a year, shed over a million hairs and drop about 25,000 poops a year. They squeeze through a very small hole, swim a long way, and hold their breath for 3 minutes. (The page gives the hole width and swim distance as fractions the OCR dropped, "an inch" and "mile," so I left out the numbers.)  
Page: 72  
The question: How athletic is a rat?  
The answer: It can fall 50 feet without getting hurt, jump 3 feet high and leap 48 inches.

**Creature: Tasmanian devil**  
Page: 82  
The question: Is it as tough as the cartoon?  
The answer: It weighs about 20 pounds, has one of the strongest grips among animals, and a bite stronger than a hyena's or a lion's.

**Creature: Camel**  
Page: 83  
The question: Do humps hold water?  
The answer: No, they hold fat. A camel can drink up to 30 gallons in less than 15 minutes.

**Creature: Beaver**  
Page: 85  
The question: Why are beaver teeth orange?  
The answer: The enamel has iron in it, which makes the teeth nearly as strong as metal and orange.

**Creature: Snow leopard**  
Page: 86  
The question: How well can it jump?  
The answer: Great fur keeps it warm and helps it walk in snow, and its powerful hind legs let it jump up to 50 feet in one leap.

**Creature: Muntjac deer**  
Page: 92  
The question: How does it communicate?  
The answer: By scent. Glands on the sides of its mouth can inflate and open so wide they turn inside out.

**Creature: Rainbow squirrel**  
Page: 93  
The question: Is there a rainbow squirrel?  
The answer: A big multicolored squirrel (red, purple and orange) that can weigh up to 4 pounds and jump as much as 18 feet between branches.

**Creature: Uakari monkey**  
Page: 95  
The question: Why is its face red?  
The answer: It has a permanently, really red face, as if wearing red paint or a mask.

**Creature: Star-nosed mole**  
Page: 90  
The question: Can a mole smell underwater?  
The answer: Yes, the page says it can "smell" underwater.

## 🐞 Insects and bugs

**Creature: Butterfly**  
Page: 6  
The question: What do butterflies eat?  
The answer: Weird stuff: they get nutrients from blood, dung, sap, sweat, tears and rotting things. So wash your hands after holding one.

**Creature: Caterpillar**  
Page: 44 (Assassin caterpillar)  
The question: Which caterpillar is deadly?  
The answer: Its camouflaged body is covered in poisonous bristles that break off and stick to your skin, releasing poison that causes blood clots throughout your body.  
Page: 57  
The question: How do some caterpillars avoid being eaten?  
The answer: They make themselves look like bird droppings.  
Page: 58 (Geometer caterpillar)  
The question: How does one look like a twig?  
The answer: It has humps like buds and no legs sticking out, so it doesn't look like a caterpillar. It must stay motionless, and spins a silk thread to a branch for support.  
Page: 101 (Mad hatterpillar)  
The question: Which caterpillar wears a hat of old heads?  
The answer: It saves its old head casings as it sheds, stacks them on its head and uses them to swat predators.

**Creature: Locust**  
Page: 43  
The question: Why are locusts dangerous?  
The answer: When they swarm, they change color, shape and even brain behavior. A swarm can hold 40–80 million per square mile and cover up to 460 square miles, eating everything.

**Creature: Murder hornet**  
Page: 45  
The question: Why is it called that?  
The answer: It's up to 2 inches long and uses pincers to snap off the heads of bees and feed the bodies to its babies. Many stings can kill a person.

**Creature: Kissing bug**  
Page: 46  
The question: Why is it called a "kissing" bug?  
The answer: It bites around the eyes, face and mouth, and poops into the bite, transmitting a parasite that spreads disease.

**Creature: Flea**  
Page: 47  
The question: How dangerous are fleas?  
The answer: They spread the bubonic plague in Europe, where 1 in 4 people died, and Japan used infected fleas in WWII.

**Creature: Fruit fly**  
Page: 55  
The question: Do fruit flies dance?  
The answer: They do a dance with exactly 7 steps, and if any step isn't done perfectly, they won't have babies.

**Creature: Praying mantis**  
Page: 63  
The question: Which insect can turn its head 360 degrees?  
The answer: The page says only the praying mantis.

**Creature: Wasp**  
Page: 64 (Paper wasp)  
The question: Who invented paper?  
The answer: Wasps knew how to make it before people did, by mixing bits of wood with the spit in their mouth to make a mixture that dries stiff.  
Page: 80 (Tarantula hawk)  
The question: Is it a spider or a hawk?  
The answer: Neither, it's a wasp, with one of the two most painful stings a person can get from a bug.  
Page: 96 (Jewel wasp)

The answer: It injects venom into the brain of a cockroach, buries it alive, and lays eggs in the grave so the babies feed on it.  
Page: 99 (Thistledown velvet ant)  
The question: Is it an ant?  
The answer: No, it's a wingless desert wasp wrapped in "velvet" fuzz, with a dangerous stinger.

**Creature: Mayfly**  
Page: 65  
The question: How long does a mayfly take to grow up?  
The answer: Eggs hatch in the water after two weeks, and the nymphs wait 3 years to become adults.

**Creature: Mountain stone weta**  
Page: 79  
The question: Can an insect survive freezing?  
The answer: Up to 80% of its body can freeze and it survives, thanks to a special ingredient in its blood.

**Creature: Mosquito**  
Page: 70  
The question: What is the most dangerous animal?  
The answer: The mosquito, killing as many as 2 million people a year. The page also lists lions, scorpions, elephants (over 500 deaths) and hippos.

## 🐊 Reptiles and amphibians

**Creature: Turtle**  
Page: 4  
The question: Is a turtle's shell its house?  
The answer: No. The shell is part of its skeleton. Turtles breathe through their rear end, and when they hibernate they use anaerobic respiration.  
Page: 17  
The question: How do temperatures decide a turtle's sex?  
The answer: Eggs incubated at 86°F produce females, 77°F produce males, and 77–86°F give mixed results.

**Creature: Python**  
Page: 7  
The question: How big a meal can a python swallow?  
The answer: Things much larger than itself. Some have swallowed people and even a whole deer.

**Creature: Snake**  
Page: 10 (Snake dens)  
The question: How many snakes gather in one den?  
The answer: Dens can number in the tens of thousands. In mating season a single female may have as many as 100 males wrap around her in a ball.  
Page: 50 (Boomslang)  
The question: How does it hide?  
The answer: It looks so much like a tree branch that birds have landed on it, thinking it's a stick.  
Page: 61 (Hognose snake)  
The question: Which snake plays dead?  
The answer: It flattens its head like a cobra, and if that fails it rolls over, lets its tongue hang out and gives off the stink of a dead animal.  
Page: 78 (Black mamba)  
The question: How fast is it?  
The answer: It can slither over 12 mph, probably faster than you can run, but it would rather run away from you.

**Creature: Frog**  
Page: 23 (Red-eyed tree frog)  
The question: Can eggs hatch early?  
The answer: They can hatch days early if they sense danger.  
Page: 88  
The question: Do frogs sleep with their eyes open?  
The answer: Frogs don't have eyelids like ours, so they seem to sleep with open eyes, and a special membrane keeps stuff out.

**Creature: Axolotl**  
Page: 26  
The question: Can an axolotl rebuild its brain?  
The answer: Yes. It can grow new limbs and also rebuild its brain.

**Creature: Marine iguana**  
Page: 35

**Creature: Komodo dragon**

The answer: It grows to 10 feet and about 150 pounds, can eat up to 80% of its body weight in one meal, and vomits out hair, bone and scales.

**Creature: Slipper snail**

**Creature: Leafy sea dragon**

## What the book shows overall

- **It's the "gross, odd and dangerous" book.** There are runs on danger (pages 43–47: locust, assassin caterpillar, murder hornet, kissing bug, flea), on mating oddities (pages 53–55: funnel-web, drunk spider, fruit fly dance), on camouflage (pages 56–61 and 97), and on sex changes (pages 13–17).
- **It has the most explicit "scatter" of mostly one-page topics.** 101 pages cover about 95 different animals, so there are few repeats.
- **It refers back to other books.** Page 13 follows "We've already learned that certain fish can change from male to female," page 17 says "One of our earlier books in this series showed that an alligator's sex was determined by the temperature," and page 84 says "the African buffalo you read about earlier in this book."
- **The caption jokes are a second layer.** Examples are the wrasse "hanging with the guys" in a couple of days, the "Rip Van Snail," and the Komodo dragon told to "take it outside for the big finish."
- **Few worms.** Only page 1 (a worm brain) and page 100 (the Bobbit worm) are about worms.

## Things you may want to check

1. **Repeats from the other books.** Page 13's slipper snail and page 14's wrasse repeat pages 68–69 of the animal-world book. Page 21's prairie dogs repeat Elephants page 7 and Whales page 62, and page 19's baby elephant repeats the animal-world book page 31. Page 26's axolotl repeats Elephants page 49, page 76's stonefish repeats Whales page 14, page 97's potoo repeats Crabby page 59, and page 85's beaver teeth repeat the Crocs book page 8.
2. **Facts that look wrong or unverified.**
   - Page 20 says the "latest advice" if you find a bear is to run. Experts say not to run from a bear, and the page's advice could be dangerous.
   - Page 35 calls the marine iguana a "predator." It eats algae. It dives to about 30 m but holds its breath closer to 30–60 minutes only in extreme cases.
   - Page 37 says birds lack taste buds. Birds have fewer taste buds and don't feel the heat of capsaicin.
   - Page 63 says the praying mantis can turn its head 360 degrees. It turns about 180.
   - Page 70 says mosquitoes kill up to 2 million a year. The figure is usually 700,000–1 million.
   - Page 74's owl turning "upside down" is wrong. Owls turn up to about 270 degrees.
   - Page 75's lungfish "up to a year" is conservative, and some survive up to 4 years.
   - Page 89's beaked whale breathing claim is not firmly established, and the depth ("3/4 mile") is hard to read.
   - Page 53's funnel-web pheromone claim and page 54's "drunk spider" are unverified.
   - Page 55's seven-step fruit fly dance is a loose description.
   - Page 29's alligators giving way to manatees is an anecdote.
   - Page 71's "2,000 babies a year" for a pair of rats is an extreme estimate.
3. **Unnamed species.** Page 54's drunk spider and page 93's "rainbow squirrel" have no scientific names given. Page 28's "fruit bats" is a whole group. Page 98's and page 99's names are common names only.
4. **Typos I noticed.**
   - "Narcissa" for the Narcisse snake dens (p10)
   - "artic" (p32)
   - "leathal" (p44)
   - "paralysed" (p76)
   - "degress" (p63)
   - "mustach" (p95)
   - "concocktion" (p54)
   - "bionic" for "biological" warfare (p47)
   - "Wcohmy" (p18, the OCR scramble of a caption)
5. **OCR limits.** The text layer was clean, so I did not render pages as images, and I did not look at the illustrations. A few captions are partly garbled in the OCR (pages 16, 18, 22 and 60).
