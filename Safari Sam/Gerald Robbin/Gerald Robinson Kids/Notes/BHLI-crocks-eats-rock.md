I read all 101 pages of *Because He Liked It: Crocs, Eat Rocks!* (the file is labeled "sample layout," but it is the full book). I used the OCR text layer, and where that text was scrambled I rendered the page as an image and read it directly. Each page follows one pattern: a "Why did God do that?" header, a short fact about an animal, and a joke caption from the animal in a speech bubble. The answer is always "Because He liked it!"

The numbers below are the printed page numbers. Creatures that appear on more than one page are grouped under one heading. Each entry gives the page, the implied question, and the answer the page gives.

## 🐾 Land: mammals

**Creature: Aye-aye**  
Page: 5  
The question: How does it find grubs inside wood?  
The answer: It taps trees with one long, bony finger, listens for the echo of grubs, eats a hole in the wood and hooks the grubs out.

**Creature: Honey badger**  
Page: 6  
The question: How fearless is it?  
The answer: It kills snakes by chewing their heads in two and keeps going after honey despite bee stings. Some have been found stung to death inside the hive.

**Creature: Cat**  
Page: 14  
The question: How do cats land on their feet?  
The answer: They have 40 more bones than humans and a very flexible spine, so they can spin in the air.  
Page: 86  
The question: How do cats walk differently?  
The answer: People and most animals walk on heels and toes, but cats walk mainly on their toes.  
Page: 94 (Van cat)  
The question: Is there a cat that likes water?  
The answer: Yes. The Van cat loves to swim and is nicknamed the "swimming cat." The breed began near Van Lake in Turkey. Its eyes are born blue and change to amber or darker blue, and some have one of each.

**Creature: Elephant**  
Page: 27  
The question: How do elephants talk over long distances?  
The answer: In sounds too low for people to hear, sent through their feet into the ground. Other elephants feel the vibrations.  
Page: 28  
The question: Can an elephant jump?  
The answer: No. It's the only land mammal unable to jump.  
Page: 29  
The question: Can elephants swim?  
The answer: Yes, they're great swimmers. They use the trunk like a snorkel and breathe through it with the mouth underwater.  
Page: 30  
The question: Is a tusk a horn or an antler?  
The answer: Neither. It's made of the same material as a tooth.

**Creature: Giraffe**  
Page: 42  
The question: How does its heart work with such a long neck?  
The answer: Its heart is the size of a basketball. When it drinks, it spreads its legs to lower the heart level so blood won't rush out of its brain. A special pad at the base of the brain keeps blood supplied so it doesn't get dizzy.

**Creature: Tree kangaroo**  
Page: 46  
The question: Do all kangaroos live on the ground?  
The answer: No. Tree kangaroos live in trees, and some in New Guinea can leap to the ground from as high as 55 feet.

**Creature: Ring-tailed lemur**  
Page: 47  
The question: What odd things does it do?  
The answer: It rubs its tail on scent glands under its arm and throws "stink bombs" at rivals. The scent also marks territory.

**Creature: Llama**  
Page: 49  
The question: What does a llama do when it's mad?  
The answer: It spits, even on another llama. A llama covered in another's half-eaten grass won't eat the rest of the day.

**Creature: Okapi**  
Page: 59  
The question: What's odd about its tongue?  
The answer: It looks a little like a deer, a llama and a zebra, and its tongue is long enough to wash its eyelids and clean its ears.

**Creature: Rhino**  
Page: 71  
The question: How well can it see?  
The answer: Very poorly. Its horn can grow 6 feet long, but it's so near-sighted that some have charged at a butterfly.

**Creature: Shrew**  
Page: 77  
The question: How fast is its heart?  
The answer: 1,200 beats a minute, against 70–75 for a grown-up and 90–110 for a child. It eats 3 times its body weight each day.

**Creature: Tasmanian devil**  
Page: 88  
The question: How strong is it?  
The answer: It's about the size of a small house cat, but it has the third most powerful bite in the animal kingdom.

**Creature: Chamois**  
Page: 95  
The question: What happens to a baby chamois if its mother dies?  
The answer: It looks like an antelope and is a fast, agile climber. Other females in the group take the baby in and care for it.

**Creature: Dog**  
Page: 96 (Puppies)  
The question: Why do puppies wrestle, and who wins?  
The answer: Play-wrestling teaches them skills they need. Sometimes the male pups let the female pups win even when the males are stronger.

**Creature: Elephant shrew**  
Page: 97  
The question: Why that name?  
The answer: Its long nose is like an elephant's trunk and it uses it to smell food and probe tight places. It looks like a rodent but is more related to an elephant than to a shrew.

**Creature: Fennec fox**  
Page: 99  
The question: Why such big ears?  
The answer: It has the largest ears of any fox. They radiate heat and keep it cool in hot weather.

**Creature: Hyena**  
Page: 100  
The question: How smart is it?  
The answer: About as smart as a primate. They live and hunt in groups, have very powerful jaws, and "talk" with laughs, growls and whoops.

**Creature: Siberian dwarf hamster**  
Page: 26  
The question: What kind of dad is it?  
The answer: A doting one. He helps with the birth, and if a baby leaves the nest he brings it back.

## 🌊 Water's edge and semi-aquatic

**Creature: Beaver**  
Page: 8  
The question: Why are its teeth reddish?  
The answer: There is iron in its teeth, the way iron makes our blood red.

**Creature: Hippo**  
Page: 43  
The question: How does it get around in deep water?  
The answer: It isn't a good swimmer. Its body is too dense to float, so it sinks to the river bottom and bounces up with its webbed feet.

**Creature: Giant otter**  
Page: 61  
The question: How does it stay dry underwater?  
The answer: Its fur is so thick it's waterproof, and the inner coat holds a layer of air. It also eats piranha.

## 🪽 Air: birds

**Creature: Albatross (and terns)**  
Page: 2  
The question: How far can they fly?  
The answer: A distance equal to the North Pole to the South Pole. One albatross flew 3,150 miles in 12 days, and another was found 6,000 miles from home.

**Creature: Cleaner birds (white egret and Egyptian plover)**  
Page: 10  
The question: Which birds clean other animals?  
The answer: The egret removes ticks from a water buffalo with its beak, and the Egyptian plover cleans the teeth of a Nile crocodile without being eaten.

**Creature: Buzzard**  
Page: 13  
The question: Why is the buzzard built the way it is?  
The answer: It can eat more than it can fly with, so it stays near its food. Its strong stomach acid kills bacteria on the carcass. Its bald head keeps feathers clean, and it wets its own legs to cool down. It smells dead animals under leaves, throws up on visitors to protect its nest, and has been seen by jet pilots at 30,000 ft.

**Creature: Birds (general)**  
Page: 9  
The question: How did God make birds light enough to fly?  
The answer: Their bones are hollow, with "trusses" inside to keep them strong.  
Page: 18 (Hummingbird, owl, condor)  
The question: Are all birds equally fast?  
The answer: No. Hovering hummingbirds beat their wings 60 times a second, owls are slower, and condors flap very little and glide on rising hot air.  
Page: 22 (Geese in V-formation)  
The question: Why fly in a "V"?  
The answer: The birds in back ride the air-wake of the birds in front and save energy, like surfing on air.  
Page: 24 (Not named)  
The question: How do heavy birds take off from water?  
The answer: Some run across the water to build enough momentum to take off.  
Page: 84 (Pelicans, geese, ducks, shore birds, storks)  
The question: How high do migrating birds fly?  
The answer: Pelicans, geese and ducks fly at 8,000–9,000 ft, shore birds at 12,000 ft, and storks at 20,000 ft.

**Creature: Anhinga**  
Page: 23  
The question: Why does it sun itself with its wings out?  
The answer: Its feathers aren't waterproof, so it spreads its wings to dry. It's called the "snake-bird" because when it swims only its head sticks up.

**Creature: Mandarin duck**  
Page: 53  
The question: How do ducklings leave the nest?  
The answer: The eggs are laid in hollowed-out spots in tall trees, so the chicks make a long fall to the ground and then follow their mother to the water. The males are brightly colored, and the ducks don't mate well with other ducks.

**Creature: Oilbird**  
Page: 58  
The question: How does it fly at night?  
The answer: It has "sonar," like a bat and a dolphin, so it navigates by sound waves in total darkness.

**Creature: Canary and ostrich**  
Page: 60  
The question: How long do birds live?  
The answer: Some birds live only 3–4 years, but a canary can live about 25 and an ostrich up to 50.

**Creature: Parrot**  
Page: 63  
The question: How long does a parrot live?  
The answer: A very long time. The Maharajah of Nawangar had a 115-year-old parrot that traveled with him in a Rolls Royce and had its own passport. Parrots also bond for life, and some die shortly after their partner.

**Creature: Emperor penguin**  
Page: 64  
The question: How deep can it dive?  
The answer: Some have been recorded at 900 feet underwater.

**Creature: Roadrunner**  
Page: 70  
The question: What can a roadrunner do?  
The answer: It's quick enough to catch and eat rattlesnakes, prefers running to flying (about 17 mph), and doesn't need "potty breaks." A nasal gland gets rid of excess salt instead of the urinary tract.

**Creature: European swift**  
Page: 85  
The question: How far can it fly in a day?  
The answer: As much as 500 miles when finding food for its babies, the same as a car going 60 mph for about 8 hours.

**Creature: Woodpecker**  
Page: 90  
The question: What's special about its tongue?  
The answer: It's several times longer than its mouth, so it wraps around the head and the tip rests in the right nostril. The pointed tip spears grubs, and backward-facing barbs keep the bug on.

## 🐞 Insects and bugs

**Creature: Butterfly**  
Page: 12 (Alcon blue)  
The question: How does a butterfly get others to raise its babies?  
The answer: The larvae have a chemical coating like a local ant's. Ants find them, take them back to the colony and feed them until they grow up and leave.  
Page: 17  
The question: What can a butterfly see and do?  
The answer: It sees colors and patterns the human eye can't, and is found on every continent except Antarctica. Scales like roof tiles make its colors, some can fly 30 mph, some have eyespots to fool predators, and some are poisonous from toxins in the plants they eat.

**Creature: Bee**  
Page: 20 (African bees)  
The question: Why are African bees so dangerous?  
The answer: A normal honey bee may sting a trespasser up to 30 times, but African bees can send as many as 30,000 stingers and chase a victim as far as a mile.  
Page: 98  
The question: What sounds do bees make?  
The answer: A "whoop" sound, a bit like saying "excuse me," and a wing-buzz that researchers call a "stop" signal to interrupt another bee.

**Creature: Fairy fly**  
Page: 31  
The question: How small is it?  
The answer: Less than one-hundredth of an inch, small enough to pass through the eye of a needle.

**Creature: Flea**  
Page: 35  
The question: How far can it jump?  
The answer: 200 times its body length. A person who could do that could jump over a 70-story building.

**Creature: Insect (general)**  
Page: 45  
The question: What's different about insect blood?  
The answer: It has no red blood cells and is colorless. It may look yellow or greenish depending on what the insect eats.

**Creature: Praying mantis**  
Page: 54  
The question: Is it "preying" or "praying"?  
The answer: "Praying," because it holds its front legs as if praying. It's a predator, it looks like a stick, and the female bites the male's head off after "dating."

**Creature: No-see-ums**  
Page: 56  
The question: Whose wings beat fastest?  
The answer: These tiny midges beat their wings 1,046 times a second, or 62,000 times a minute, the record for the fastest fluttering wings. They're called "no-see-ums" because they're so small and fast.

**Creature: Giant water bug**  
Page: 89  
The question: How does it catch fish?  
The answer: It grows to about 8 inches, hangs upside down in the water with a snorkel, and can eat teen-age piranha. It injects digestive juices and sips the fish's insides through a straw.

**Creature: Firefly**  
Page: 93  
The question: Is a firefly a fly?  
The answer: No, it's a beetle. It's bioluminescent, and each species has its own flash pattern, like different languages.

## 🕷️ Spiders and other crawlers

**Creature: Spider**  
Page: 21  
The question: What if the "spider" you found was already dead?  
The answer: It may only be the skin a spider left behind after outgrowing it. Some spiders shed skin like snakes, and a bigger spider may be nearby.  
Page: 25 (Spitting spider, Scytodes)  
The question: How does it catch prey?  
The answer: It spits a "glue" that sticks and is poisonous, swaying side to side to cover the prey in a crisscrossed "Z" pattern.  
Page: 36  
The question: How does a spider eat if it can't digest solid food?  
The answer: It bites its prey and injects a poison with digestive liquid, which turns the victim's body into a "soup."  
Page: 44  
The question: How can a spider go so long without food?  
The answer: It has a very low metabolism. One variety can live over a year without eating.  
Page: 87 (Tarantula)  
The question: Why is the tarantula famous?  
The answer: It's one of the most feared spiders. Long ago, people in Italy bitten by one would leap about uncontrollably, and the Tarantella dance and music are named for it.

**Creature: Velvet worm**  
Page: 92  
The question: How does a slow animal catch food?  
The answer: It has two "pistols" or hoses, one on each side of its head, that shoot streaming gobs of glue at prey. The glue dries and holds the bug until the worm gets there.

## 🐊 Reptiles and amphibians

**Creature: Crocodile**  
Page: 1 (Crocs Eat Rocks)  
The question: Why do crocs swallow stones?  
The answer: To help digestion and add weight so they can stay underwater, as long as an hour. An average croc has 10–15 stones in its stomach and eats very little because it's slow-moving and cold-blooded.  
Page: 40 (Australian freshwater crocodile)  
The question: Can a crocodile gallop?  
The answer: Yes. It swims, crawls and walks, but can also gallop like a horse at 15 mph for a short distance. (The page calls it an alligator.)

**Creature: Frog**  
Page: 11 (Tadpole)  
The question: What does a dying tadpole do for the others?  
The answer: It squeezes out a body fluid that warns other tadpoles of danger and triggers a quick transformation that helps them grow larger.  
Page: 32  
The question: How do frogs breathe and eat?  
The answer: They breathe with their mouths closed, and some take in air through their back or hairlike projections on their hind legs. Their prey capture is the fastest known, about 1/6000 of a second, too fast for the human eye.  
Page: 37 (Golden tree frog, Japanese flying frog, Wallace's flying frog)  
The question: Can frogs glide?  
The answer: Yes. They spread their webbed feet like a parachute. Wallace's flying frog has been known to jump from 250 feet up a tree and glide 250 feet forward while dropping.

**Creature: Salamander**  
Page: 33 (Fire salamander)  
The question: How does it defend itself?  
The answer: It spits a noxious fluid that can cause temporary blindness.  
Page: 55 (California newt)  
The question: How poisonous is it?  
The answer: Just one drop of its nerve poison can kill 7,000 mice.  
Page: 72  
The question: How does it catch bugs?  
The answer: Its tongue is called the world's most explosive muscle and shoots out like an arrow from a crossbow. It's also elastic and can stretch longer than its body.  
Page: 73  
The question: What can it regrow?  
The answer: Any limb it loses. Doctors and scientists are studying whether people could do the same.

**Creature: Snake**  
Page: 38 (Flying snake)  
The question: Can a snake fly?  
The answer: One can jump from a tree and flatten its body like a ribbon to "float" to the ground from as far as 500 feet away.  
Page: 75 (Sand-swimming snake)  
The question: How does it travel on sand?  
The answer: It "swims" just under the surface of the sand instead of traveling on top.  
Page: 80  
The question: How does a snake get water?  
The answer: It can't drink, so it gets its water from the animals it eats. The page ties this to the Bible's line that the snake would "eat dust."  
Page: 81 (Death adder)  
The question: How does it hunt?  
The answer: It uses the tip of its tail to copy a grub's movement. When a lizard goes for the "grub," the adder strikes.

**Creature: Lizard**  
Page: 39 (Frilled-neck lizard)  
The question: Can a lizard run on two legs?  
The answer: Yes. The frilled-neck lizard of Australia runs on 2 legs, and when frightened it opens its mouth and unfolds its frill to look much larger.  
Page: 41 (Flying gecko and Draco lizard)  
The question: Can lizards glide?  
The answer: Yes. The flying gecko jumps out of trees and glides, and the Draco lizard can "fly" the length of 2 football fields.  
Page: 68 (Tuatara)  
The question: How long does it incubate its eggs?  
The answer: Up to 425 days, over a year and 2 months. It lives in New Zealand.

## 🐋 Sea mammals

**Creature: Whale**  
Page: 7  
The question: What's hidden about whales before birth?  
The answer: They have back legs in the womb that disappear before birth.  
Page: 91 (Blue whale)  
The question: How big is the blue whale?  
The answer: It's believed to be the largest animal ever, with a tongue that weighs as much as an elephant. The largest dinosaur weighed 30–35 tons, and the blue whale is 3–5 times that. Its heart can weigh 1,000 pounds.

**Creature: Dolphin**  
Page: 16  
The question: How do dolphins make sounds?  
The answer: They have no vocal cords and no sense of taste. They make clicks and whistles with air channels and cavities in their heads.  
Page: 65 (Boto, pink river dolphin)  
The question: How does it find its way in muddy water?  
The answer: It's almost blind, so it swims on its side and uses a fin to feel the bottom.

## 🐟 Fish and sharks

**Creature: Angler fish**  
Page: 3  
The question: How does it lure prey?  
The answer: It has a light on a stalk from its forehead, like a fishing pole with a light as bait. Many creatures of the sea are bioluminescent, and light is used as a signal and a lure in the dark.

**Creature: Archer fish**  
Page: 4  
The question: How does it catch bugs?  
The answer: It squirts a high-pressure jet of water up to 6 feet to knock bugs off branches, allowing for the bending of light in water and the pull of gravity. Sometimes it jumps out of the water with its tail instead.

**Creature: Shark**  
Page: 15  
The question: How dangerous are sharks?  
The answer: Shark attacks are very rare. The page says chiropractors kill more people than sharks every year.

**Creature: Reef fish**  
Page: 19 (Not named)  
The question: Can a fish change sex?  
The answer: In groups of hundreds, most are female. If the male is removed, the lead female replaces him in every way. In less than a week she changes color and becomes a fully functioning male.

**Creature: Lionfish**  
Page: 48  
The question: How dangerous is it?  
The answer: Its venomous spines are deadly to prey but not to humans. A puncture causes severe pain and possibly headaches or vomiting, but you won't die.

**Creature: Piranha**  
Page: 66  
The question: How dangerous are piranha?  
The answer: A school of about 20 can strip a fish or animal to the bone in minutes, but only 4 of the 20-some varieties are ferocious. They pose little danger to people, prefer hurt or crippled prey, and clean up the dead, like water buzzards.

**Creature: Pufferfish**  
Page: 69  
The question: Why does it puff up?  
The answer: When scared it fills itself with air to look bigger and scare off predators.

**Creature: Salmon**  
Page: 74  
The question: How does it find its way home?  
The answer: It's born in a stream, swims to the ocean and back to the same stream by smell, not by map or compass. If it loses its sense of smell, it can get lost.

**Creature: Amazon leaping fish**  
Page: 83 (Not named)  
The question: How does it lay its eggs?  
The answer: A male and female jump out of the water onto a leaf and stick to it while she lays eggs, then slide back in. For two days the male splashes the eggs with his tail to keep them wet.

## 🦀 Sea invertebrates

**Creature: Single-cell glowing organism**  
Page: 34  
The question: How does a tiny organism get revenge?  
The answer: When a shrimp eats it, it glows even inside the shrimp, which alerts a cuttlefish that eats shrimp. The tiny organism may be eaten, but the animal that ate it gets eaten too.

**Creature: Lobster**  
Page: 50  
The question: Why won't a lobster fight the same opponent twice?  
The answer: The winner's smell stays on the loser's antenna, which is how the loser recognizes and remembers it. If he loses the antenna, he'll fight again by mistake.  
Page: 51 (Crustaceans)  
The question: Can crustaceans grow back lost limbs?  
The answer: Yes, they can regrow a lost limb, and lobsters can even detach a claw at will.  
Page: 52 (Spiny lobster)  
The question: Do lobsters migrate?  
The answer: Yes. Spiny lobsters off Florida migrate about 30 miles south when the water gets cold, walking in single file in groups up to 60, up to 10 miles a day.

**Creature: Octopus**  
Page: 57  
The question: How smart is it with a small brain?  
The answer: Its brain is about the size of a walnut but it learns quickly, and given a jar with a crab it can learn to open the lid with one arm. It has 3 hearts, and brain cells are found throughout its arms.

**Creature: Oyster**  
Page: 62  
The question: Can an oyster change sex?  
The answer: Yes, often. It can be a boy, turn into a girl, and back into a boy again.

**Creature: Shrimp**  
Page: 67 (Pistol shrimp)  
The question: How does it stun prey?  
The answer: It snaps a claw to make a shock wave or "sound bullet." The snap makes bubbles that collapse and briefly reach the temperature of the Sun.  
Page: 78 (Mantis shrimp)  
The question: How strong is a tiny shrimp?  
The answer: It has 16 types of color-analyzing cells (people have 3) to track prey, and two "clubs" on its feet that hit with the force of a .22 caliber bullet to crack a crab's shell.

**Creature: Sea slugs**  
Page: 76  
The question: Can an animal be solar powered?  
The answer: Yes. Two groups of sea slugs convert the sun's energy into sugars and other nutrients.

**Creature: Snail**  
Page: 79  
The question: Why do snails carry their homes?  
The answer: The page jokes it's because it would take too long to crawl back each night. Some live on land and some in water, and those in water have gills. Their eyes are on the "stalks" on the head, and land snails have both male and female parts.

**Creature: Giant spider crab**  
Page: 82  
The question: How big is it?  
The answer: Its legs stretch 13 feet from tip to tip, longer than a person is tall and reaching from the floor to the ceiling and half up the room above.

## 🔥 Tiny and extreme creatures

**Creature: Heat-loving creatures**  
Page: 101  
The question: How much heat can a creature stand?  
The answer: A South African desert beetle (weevil) can stand 126°F, and a single-cell microbe can live at ocean vents over 250°F, hotter than boiling.

## What the book shows overall

- **This book is a "design and survival tricks" collection.** Many pages are about animals that do something their body shouldn't allow: fly without wings (flying frog, flying snake, Draco lizard, flying gecko), fool predators or prey (death adder, mantis), or use odd weapons (spitting spider, velvet worm, pistol shrimp, archer fish).
- **Whole runs of pages share a theme.** Pages 22–24 (V-formation, anhinga, running on water), 27–30 (elephants), 37–41 (gliders and runners), 75–81 (sand snake, sea slugs, shrew, shrimp, snail, snakes) and 87–93 (tarantula to firefly).
- **From about page 31 on, the pages run roughly A to Z:** Fairy fly, Frog, Fire salamander, Flea, Flying frog, Flying snake, Frilled lizard, Flying gecko, Giraffe, Hippo, Insect, Kangaroo, Lemur, Lionfish, Llama, Lobster, Mandarin duck, Mantis, No-see-ums, Octopus, Oilbird, Okapi, Otter, Oyster, Parrot, Penguin, Piranha, Pistol shrimp, Pufferfish, Roadrunner, Rhino, Salamander, Salmon, Sea slug, Shrew, Shrimp, Snail, Snake, Spider crab, Swift, Tarantula, Tasmanian devil, Water bug, Woodpecker, Whale and Worm.
- **The caption jokes are a second layer.** Examples are the octopus's "I can play a duet with myself" and the pistol shrimp in a cowboy hat.
- **Some Bible ties appear.** Page 7 mentions the famous Bible story of the whale, and page 80 explains the snake that "eats dust."

## Things you may want to check

1. **Unnamed animals.** Page 19's reef fish, page 24's running-on-water bird and page 83's Amazon fish are not named on the page.
2. **Fact checks.**
   - Page 15's claim that chiropractors kill more people than sharks every year is unsupported and could be removed or softened.
   - Page 40 calls the Australian freshwater crocodile an "alligator." Australia has no alligators.
   - Page 41's claim that the Draco lizard can glide the length of 2 football fields looks too long. Real Draco glides are about 30–60 m (up to about half a football field).
   - Page 10's story of the Egyptian plover cleaning crocodile teeth is an old legend that is disputed.
   - Page 13 says a buzzard "poops" acid on the carcass to kill bacteria. Vultures do use stomach acid to survive carrion, but the idea that it cleans the carcass is unsupported.
   - Page 63's 115-year-old parrot is an anecdote, not a documented record.
3. **Typos I noticed.**
   - "insistant" (p6)
   - "Egyption" (p10)
   - "Lllama" (p49)
   - "Jaban" and "Walace's" (p37)
   - "minature" (p37)
   - "Anihinga" (p23)
   - "temperture" (p67)
   - "Emporer" (p64)
   - "murkey" (p65)
   - "restaraunts" (p87)
   - "visitor" misspelled as "vistor" (p13)
   - "Antartica" on the page 17 sign
4. **OCR limits.** About 60 pages had scrambled text, and I checked those against the page images. Where I wrote "(Not named)," the page itself doesn't name the animal.
