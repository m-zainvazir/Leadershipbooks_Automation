## Summary

*Where Did Grandma Go?* is a short rhyming picture book for children who have lost a grandparent. It is by Gerald Robison, with art by Fred Sayers (so, as far as I can tell, it is not part of the series). It runs 25 numbered story pages, and the other pages are front matter and a back advert.

- **Story:** A young child (hair and overalls, no name or gender given) searches the house and the neighborhood for Grandma. The family has gathered for her funeral, and no adult will explain. The child learns Grandma has died and asks where she is.
- **Resolution:** On a park bench, a robed figure the text calls "the angel" (the pictures draw a long-haired, bearded man in a white robe) tells the child he has put Grandma "somewhere deep down inside," in a "very treasured place" made of her memories, hugs and kisses. The child finally understands: "the angel hid her in my heart."
- **Message:** The child's sense of loss turns into a comforting idea. Grandma lives on in memory and love, and it's God (the angel) who put her there. The book is gentle but clearly faith-based.

**There are no animals in this book.** The only living "figures" are people and a spiritual one:

- The child (main character).
- Grandma, shown realistically and as a faint ghost-like image.
- Grandpa, mourners, a mother-like woman in blue and other family guests.
- The "angel," drawn as a white-robed man.
- Plants: a flowering plant the child kneels by on page 16, and flowers in vases on page 7.
- Metaphors that name living things: the child asks if Grandma is "planted like a seed" who will "wake up one day" (page 15).

## Areas for curiosity (good questions to ask with a child)

1. **What does "died" mean?** The child hears "the doctors took her" and "God took her" and doesn't understand. Ask what the child understands and what words feel scary or confusing.
2. **Why did the grown-ups say "go play"?** Page 10 shows adults excluding the child. Ask how it feels to be left out when something big happens.
3. **What does "here in spirit" mean?** (page 14) and "planted like a seed" (page 15). These are two very different ideas, and the child is trying them out.
4. **Where do we keep people we love?** Pages 21–25 answer "deep down inside." Ask what memories the child has of Grandma and where in the body they feel them.
5. **The things on the pages.** Ask the child about the sewing machine, rocking chair, bike and photo, and what each one reminds them of.
6. **Grandpa's eyes (page 9).** The child sees sadness in Grandpa. Ask how the child can tell when someone is sad, and how they could help.
7. **Who is the "angel"?** The text never says it's Jesus or God. It only says "angel." The illustrations draw a man who looks like traditional pictures of Jesus.
8. **The title question.** "Where did Grandma go?" is asked on page 1 and answered on page 25. Ask the child what they thought the answer would be before the end.

## Things you may want to check

1. **Faith-specific message.** The book teaches a Christian view: God "took her home," an "angel" hides her in the heart, and the figure drawn looks like Jesus. It never mentions heaven by name. If this will be shared with children of other beliefs, you may want to note that.
2. **Possible mixed message.** Page 12 says God took Grandma home, but page 17–25 says the angel put her in the child's heart. The child's last question ("why can't I take her home with me?") is answered with "she's in your heart," which is a different answer from "she's with God."
3. **"Angel" versus the picture.** The text says "angel" on pages 17, 23 and 25, while the pictures show a man in a white robe who looks like Jesus. If you want consistency, the word or the picture could change.
4. **Small text problems.**
   - Page 9 has "Byes" (the OCR shows "Eyes" for "Byes," which looks like the "Bye" pun with a capital B).
   - Page 20 ends the line "that would never do" with no period.
   - Page 11 has "ceases to be" on page 13, which some readers may find harsh.
5. **The OCR text layer.** The text layer is mostly doubled letters. Quotes above come from the page images, not the OCR.
6. **Not in the series.** There's no "Why did God do that?" format, no animals and no jokes. Its style (rhymed couplets and full-page illustrations) matches the *Where Did Grandpa Go?* book, which I expect is the companion piece.
