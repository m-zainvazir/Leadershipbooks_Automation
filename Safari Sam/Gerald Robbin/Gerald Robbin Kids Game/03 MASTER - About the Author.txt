Dr. Gerald D. Robison, affectionately known as "Dr. G." He has pastored churches on three continents and trained over 1,200 Bible teachers in more than 25 countries. He served as the international training manager for Walk Thru the Bible ministries, and has founded and cofounded three ministries: UnveilinGLORY, Cat N Dog Theology, and To All Nations.

He has instructed over 300 classes on World Missions, including Perspectives on the World Christian Movement, and has been a favorite speaker for Perspectives, Steeling the Mind conferences, and missions conferences in the USA and around the world. He has worked with the Issachar Initiative, Finishing the Task, Perspectives, and City for the Nations. He has served on the faculty of Bible colleges and a seminary.

He was called to Christian ministry while still in high school. He earned a BA in psychology and counseling at Furman and Mercer Universities, a master's degree and a Doctor of Ministry at Luther Rice Seminary, a second master's degree in education and counseling at Georgia State University, and did further graduate study at the International Institute of Theology and Law sponsored by Simon Greenleaf School of Law and the International Institute of Human Rights sponsored by the University of Strasbourg, France.

Following a battle with tongue cancer, he has devoted more of his time to writing.

His lifelong passion: helping reach people of every tongue, every tribe and every nation with the message of Christ's love, until representatives from every group are gathered into the throne room of God worshipping our heavenly Father. He often points to Revelation 7:9-10 as the picture of where all of this is going.

His grandchildren and great-grandchildren, named in the dedication of The Innkeeper, are Hannah, Jacob, Beth, Seth, Caleb, Emily, Gracie, Niklaus and Josiah.

=== HOW THE BOOKS FIT TOGETHER ===
All the strands do the same thing at different altitudes. The children's series teaches a five-year-old to look at a pistol shrimp and ask what God is like. The Innkeeper teaches a lifelong churchgoer to reread ten familiar verses and find a man he had been slandering. The Battle Rages On teaches a believer to stop treating the unseen world as decoration. The grief books sit with a small child who has lost a grandparent. The move is always the same: stay close to the actual words of the text, and let the details set the imagination free. As the foreword to The Innkeeper puts it — people say the devil is in the details, but the greater truth is that the divine is in the details.

EVERY BOOK IN THE SERIES CARRIES THE SAME PARENT'S GUIDE TO WONDER AND DISCOVERY, and its four instructions to parents are the heart of how these books are meant to be used:
  - Take it slowly. Let each picture and story breathe. Wonder grows best when it's not hurried.
  - Ask questions. "What do you see?" "Why do you think God made it this way?" Their answers may open new doors of discovery.
  - Follow curiosity. If your child wants to stay with one page, or skip ahead to another, let their interest guide you.
  - Bring it to life. When you see a flower, a bird, or the night sky, pause and say, "God liked this, too."

The guide closes: "My hope is that, as you read together, you will both come to see the world not as ordinary, but as extraordinary — because He liked it, and He made it good."
